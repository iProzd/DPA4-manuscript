\section{Introduction}

Machine learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs) have established themselves as near-linear-cost surrogates for density functional theory (DFT), enabling atomistic simulations at previously inaccessible time and length scales~\cite{behler2007generalized,bartok2010gaussian,zhang2018deep,batzner2022nequip,batatia2022mace}.
The recent generation of large atomistic models (LAMs) further demonstrates that a single model, trained on millions of DFT calculations, can transfer across diverse chemistry with competitive accuracy~\cite{batatia2023foundation,zhang2025dpa3}.
For example, DPA3~\cite{zhang2025dpa3} established an invariant multi-layer graph neural network on line-graph series (LiGS) with smooth and conservative energy guarantees, and showed that the generalization error of this model family follows a scaling law with training data and model size.

Despite this progress, three tensions in the current MLIP design space have become apparent.
\emph{(a) Accuracy ceiling.}
SO(3)-equivariant models such as MACE~\cite{batatia2022mace}, NequIP~\cite{batzner2022nequip}, and EquiformerV2~\cite{liao2023equiformerv2} achieve the highest accuracy on small-molecule AIMD benchmarks (rMD17, 3BPA), a gap that DPA3 itself acknowledges in its Discussion section.
However, their accuracy comes at a steep computational price: the Clebsch--Gordan tensor products required by full SO(3) equivariance scale as $\mathcal{O}(L^6)$ in the maximum spherical-harmonic order $L$, making large models and long simulations expensive.
Invariant models like DPA3 avoid this cost but leave accuracy on the table for conformationally rich molecular systems.
\emph{(b) Compute bottleneck.}
DPA3 saturates the memory of an A800 80\,GB GPU at moderate model size, and the existing implementation is incompatible with \texttt{torch.compile} due to dynamic neighbor counts, in-place graph buffer updates, and Python control flow on the hot path.
There is therefore no straightforward route to deliver matched accuracy at lower memory and higher throughput within the existing design.
\emph{(c) Physical robustness at short range.}
Existing LAMs, including DPA3 and MACE-MP-0~\cite{batatia2023foundation}, handle short-range nuclear repulsion either through data-driven extrapolation (which is unreliable under extreme conditions) or through post-hoc Ziegler--Biersack--Littmark (ZBL) wrappers applied outside the neural network.
Such wrappers introduce a discontinuity in the coupling and can break $C^2$ smoothness or the conservation property of the underlying model, rendering them unsuitable for high-energy collision and radiation-damage simulations.

Here we present DPA4, an interatomic potential architecture that addresses all three tensions simultaneously.
Our contributions are as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{SO(2)-equivariant local-frame architecture with attention.}
    For each directed edge $(i,j)$, we construct a local coordinate frame whose $z$-axis aligns with the bond direction $\hat{r}_{ij}$; the residual rotation symmetry in this frame is the abelian group SO(2), not SO(3).
    Equivariant operations under SO(2) are diagonal in the angular-order basis $\{e^{im\theta}\}$ and incur only $\mathcal{O}(d \cdot m_{\max})$ cost per edge---comparable to invariant models---while retaining the expressiveness advantage of equivariant features over multi-body tasks.
    This approach, inspired by the eSCN framework~\cite{passaro2023reducing}, is combined with multi-head attention over SO(2)-invariant ($m=0$) channels to capture long-range many-body context within each message-passing step.
    DPA4 thereby achieves accuracy competitive with full SO(3) models (MACE, eSEN~\cite{fu2025esen}) at a cost comparable to invariant models.
  \item \textbf{End-to-end \texttt{torch.compile} training.}
    The algorithmic gain from replacing CG tensor products with diagonal SO(2) operations, combined with a padded-neighbor representation that eliminates dynamic shapes, makes DPA4 fully compatible with \texttt{torch.compile}.
    The conservative-force training path requires second-order coordinate derivatives; DPA4 solves the resulting \texttt{autograd}/Inductor incompatibility by capturing the inner \texttt{autograd.grad} as ordinary FX nodes via \texttt{make\_fx} symbolic tracing before compilation.
    Together, these changes yield \textbf{2--3$\times$} wall-clock training speedup over eager execution at matched accuracy, with the model running without modification on commodity hardware (RTX 4090, V100) in addition to data-centre accelerators.
  \item \textbf{Native ZBL zone bridging.}
    DPA4 introduces a two-component \emph{Zone Bridging} framework: (i) \textbf{InnerClamp}, a $C^3$ septic Hermite distance saturation that closes the scalar-distance leakage channel, and (ii) \textbf{SFPG} (Source Freeze Propagation Gate), a per-node multiplicative gate that is zero whenever any neighbour enters the bridging window, closing both the direction channel and the multi-hop propagation channel.
    Together they guarantee that $E^{\mathrm{NN}}$ is strictly constant under motions of short-range pairs, so differentiating the total energy $E^{\mathrm{ZBL}} + E^{\mathrm{NN}}(\tilde{r})$ recovers pure ZBL forces in the bridging zone.
    The combined PES is $C^3$ in atomic coordinates everywhere.
    This design extends DPA4's domain of validity to high-energy collisions, radiation-damage cascades, and compression/melting without any post-hoc modification.
\end{enumerate}
DPA4 achieves the highest composite performance score (CPS) on Matbench Discovery~\cite{riebesell2025matbench} among compliant models, state-of-the-art energy accuracy on SPICE-MACE-OFF and TorsionNet-500, and surpasses DPA3 and MACE on small-molecule AIMD benchmarks (rMD17, 3BPA), while requiring [TODO: X$\times$] fewer GPU-hours to reach matched WBM energy MAE.
We note that DPA4 is presented here as a single-task, per-dataset model; multi-task dataset encoding and foundation-model pre-training are left to follow-up work.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:results} presents all benchmark results, beginning with the efficiency--accuracy Pareto analysis (Fig.~1), followed by Matbench Discovery and small-molecule benchmarks, training and inference efficiency, and the ZBL coupling case study.
Section~\ref{sec:methods} describes the DPA4 architecture in full mathematical detail and specifies datasets and training protocols.

\subsection{DPA4 model architecture}

Consider a system of $N$ atoms with atomic numbers $\{Z_i\}$ and positions $\{\mathbf{R}_i\}$.
DPA4 decomposes the total potential energy as a sum over atoms, $E = \sum_i E_i$, where the total energy further separates into a learned neural-network branch and an analytical ZBL branch:
\begin{equation}
  E = E^{\mathrm{NN}}(\tilde{r}) +
      \frac{1}{2}\sum_{(i,j)} E_{ij}^{\mathrm{ZBL}}(r_{ij}).
  \label{eq:total_energy}
\end{equation}
Here $r_{ij} = |\mathbf{R}_j - \mathbf{R}_i|$ is the true interatomic distance, while $\tilde{r}$ denotes effective distances after the zone-bridging pipeline (Section~\ref{sec:zbl_method}): inside the bridging window $[r_{\mathrm{in}}, r_{\mathrm{out}}]$, short-range pair distances seen by the descriptor are smoothly clamped via InnerClamp so that the NN branch is constant for any pair in the ZBL-dominant zone.
Forces and the virial tensor are obtained by automatic differentiation of $E$ with respect to $\{\mathbf{R}_i\}$ and the cell matrix, respectively, so both branches contribute conservatively.
Figure~\ref{fig:arch} provides an overview of the architecture.
Section~\ref{sec:so2} describes the SO(2)-equivariant local-frame message-passing layers; Section~\ref{sec:attn} details the attention mechanism; Section~\ref{sec:zbl_method} specifies the native ZBL zone-bridging; and Section~\ref{sec:symmetry} states the symmetry guarantees.

\subsubsection{Local frames and SO(2)-equivariant features}
\label{sec:so2}

\paragraph{Motivation.}
Full SO(3) equivariance is typically enforced via Clebsch--Gordan (CG) tensor products, whose cost scales as $\mathcal{O}(L^6)$ in the maximum spherical-harmonic order $L$~\cite{batatia2022mace,liao2023equiformerv2}.
This steep scaling forces a trade-off between model expressiveness (high $L$) and computational cost.
A key observation, exploited by Passaro and Zitnick~\cite{passaro2023reducing} in the eSCN network, is that in the local frame of a directed edge $(i,j)$ the residual symmetry is not SO(3) but the abelian subgroup SO(2): once the $z$-axis is fixed along $\hat{r}_{ij}$, any global rotation can be decomposed into a trivial alignment followed by a rotation about $z$.
Equivariant operations under SO(2) are \emph{diagonal} in the angular-order basis, reducing the per-edge cost to $\mathcal{O}(d \cdot m_{\max})$, where $d$ is the feature channel width and $m_{\max}$ is the maximum angular order---comparable to purely invariant models.
DPA4 adopts this local-frame strategy as the foundation of its message-passing layers.

\paragraph{Local frame construction.}
For each directed edge $(i,j)$, the primary axis is the unit bond vector $\mathbf{e}_3 = \hat{r}_{ij} = (\mathbf{R}_j - \mathbf{R}_i)/r_{ij}$.
Rather than using a fixed reference-vector crossproduct (which is singular at collinear configurations), DPA4 derives the full rotation from an edge-aligned unit quaternion via two complementary charts: \emph{Chart A} is regular away from the $-Z$ pole, \emph{Chart B} is regular away from the $+Z$ pole, and a $C^\infty$ normalised-linear blend (nlerp with shortest-arc sign alignment) is applied in their overlap region.
This two-chart construction is singularity-free and smooth in all edge directions.
The rotation $\mathbf{q}_{ij}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
  R(\mathbf{q}_{ij})\,\hat{r}_{ij} = (0,0,1)^{\top},
\end{equation}
mapping the global frame to one in which the bond points along $+Z$.
A random azimuthal roll $\gamma \sim \mathcal{U}[0,2\pi)$ is left-composed per edge during training ($\mathbf{q} \leftarrow \mathbf{q}_\gamma \cdot \mathbf{q}_{ij}$) to break the correlation between the arbitrary in-plane gauge and the training targets, improving generalisation without affecting equivariance.
Squared-norm clamping before square-roots and fp32 promotion for half-precision inputs guard against NaN gradients at near-zero displacements.

\paragraph{Feature representation.}
Node features $\mathbf{h}_i$ are tensors of shape $(D,\,C)$ where $D = (l_{\max}+1)^2$ indexes real spherical-harmonic coefficients $(l,m)$ with $l=0,\ldots,l_{\max}$ and $m=-l,\ldots,l$, and $C$ is the channel width.
The degree-$l$ block transforms under the $(2l+1)$-dimensional real Wigner-D representation of SO(3):
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{h}^{(l)} \;\longmapsto\; D^l(R)\,\mathbf{h}^{(l)},
  \quad l = 0, 1, \ldots, l_{\max}.
\end{equation}
The $l=0$ block is SO(3)-invariant; higher-$l$ blocks are equivariant.
For the SO(2) convolution step a truncated layout retaining only $|m| \leq m_{\max} \leq l_{\max}$ coefficients is used, reducing per-edge cost from $\mathcal{O}(D^2)$ to $\mathcal{O}(D \cdot (2m_{\max}+1))$.
Inverse-rescale compensation $\sqrt{(2l+1)/(2\min(l,m_{\max})+1)}$ is applied after the rotate-back step to restore expected degree-block norms after the truncated round-trip.

\paragraph{Message-passing equations.}
At each interaction block $\ell$, edge geometry is computed once and reused across all blocks via an edge-feature cache.
The SO(2) convolution operates in four steps.
\emph{(i) Rotate to local.}
Source features are projected to the truncated $m$-major layout:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{loc}}_{ij} =
    D^{(\leq m_{\max})}(\mathbf{q}_{ij})\,\mathbf{h}_j^{(\ell)},
\end{equation}
where $D^{(\leq m_{\max})}$ is the row-subset of the Wigner-D matrix retaining only $|m| \leq m_{\max}$.
\emph{(ii) Radial modulation.}
Each coefficient at degree $l$ is multiplied by a degree-specific radial feature $\rho^{(\ell)}_l(r_{ij})$ produced by a RadialMLP (sinusoidal basis with trainable frequencies, followed by a $C^3$-continuous cutoff envelope):
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{loc}}_{ij,l} \;\leftarrow\;
    \rho^{(\ell)}_l(r_{ij}) \cdot \mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{loc}}_{ij,l}.
\end{equation}
\emph{(iii) SO(2)-equivariant mixing.}
An SO2Linear layer applies an unconstrained linear map to the $m=0$ block and a 2$\times$2 complex coupling to each $(\text{-}m, +m)$ pair, preserving equivariance diagonally without CG products.
When $n_{\mathrm{focus}} > 1$, multiple parallel streams (\emph{multi-focus}) process the same edge context; a label-smoothed softmax competition on $l=0$ scalars re-weights the streams before the rotate-back, allowing the model to specialize sub-channels to different geometric motifs.
\emph{(iv) Rotate back and aggregate.}
Messages are rotated back to the global frame and scatter-summed to destination nodes:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{h}_i^{(\ell+1)} = \mathbf{h}_i^{(\ell)}
    + f_{\mathrm{upd}}^{(\ell)}\!\left(
      \sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}(i)}
        D^{(\leq m_{\max})\top}(\mathbf{q}_{ij})\,
        \sqrt{\tfrac{2l+1}{2\min(l,m_{\max})+1}}\;
        \mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{loc}}_{ij}
    \right).
  \label{eq:mp}
\end{equation}
The update $f_{\mathrm{upd}}^{(\ell)}$ is an equivariant FFN with per-degree gates on higher-$l$ channels derived from $l=0$ scalars (Section~\ref{sec:attn}).
The atom-level energy contribution is read out from the $l=0$, $m=0$ channel of the final node feature through a scalar MLP: $E_i^{\mathrm{NN}} = \mathrm{MLP}(\mathbf{h}_i^{(L_{\mathrm{blk}})}|_{l=0})$.
DPA4 additionally supports \emph{pyramid schedules} in which $l_{\max}$ and $m_{\max}$ decrease across blocks, trading off early-block expressiveness for late-block efficiency.

\subsubsection{Attention}
\label{sec:attn}

\paragraph{Motivation.}
Plain degree-normalized scatter aggregation weights neighbors by learned radial functions but cannot modulate relevance dynamically based on the central atom's chemical context.
This limits many-body expressiveness in heterogeneous environments such as catalytic surfaces, disordered alloys, or zeolite frameworks.

\paragraph{Design.}
Attention in DPA4 operates on the $l=0$ (SO(3)-invariant) subspace of the aggregated edge messages, so attention weights are rotationally invariant and do not break equivariance of higher-$l$ channels.
At each block $\ell$, queries and keys are projected from the scalar features of the destination and source atoms, augmented by a radial logit from the same RadialMLP:
\begin{align}
  e_{ij}^{(\ell)} &= \frac{\mathbf{q}_i^{(\ell)\top}
                            \mathbf{k}_j^{(\ell)}}{\sqrt{d_k}}
                  + b_{\mathrm{rad}}(r_{ij}),
\end{align}
where $\mathbf{q}_i = W_Q\,\mathrm{RMSNorm}(\mathbf{h}_i|_{l=0})$, $\mathbf{k}_j = W_K\,\mathrm{RMSNorm}(\mathbf{h}_j|_{l=0})$, and $b_{\mathrm{rad}}$ is a scalar projection of the radial basis.

The attention weight uses an \emph{envelope-gated} softmax that guarantees the weight decays smoothly to zero at the cutoff:
\begin{equation}
  \alpha_{ij}^{(\ell)} =
    \frac{s(r_{ij})^2 \cdot \exp\!\left(
      e_{ij}^{(\ell)} - \max_{k} e_{ik}^{(\ell)}
    \right)}
    {\mathrm{softplus}(\zeta)\,
      e^{-\max_k e_{ik}^{(\ell)}}
     + \displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathcal{N}(i)}
       s(r_{ik})^2\,e^{e_{ik}^{(\ell)} - \max_k e_{ik}^{(\ell)}}},
  \label{eq:attn_weight}
\end{equation}
where $s(r)$ is the $C^3$ cutoff envelope, $\zeta$ is a learnable positive bias (initialized to $\mathrm{softplus}^{-1}(1)$) that prevents division-by-zero when all edges carry negligible envelope weight near the cutoff boundary, and the denominator shift by $\exp(-\max_k)$ is the standard log-sum-exp stabilization.
Zero-weight padding edges are excluded from the group-max computation by setting their logit to $-\infty$ before the reduction.

After scatter-summing the attention-weighted $l=0$ messages, an output-side head gate modulates each attention head based on the destination atom's scalar feature:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{a}_i^{(\ell)} \;\leftarrow\;
    \mathbf{a}_i^{(\ell)}
    \odot \sigma\!\left(
      W_G\,\mathrm{RMSNorm}(\mathbf{h}_i|_{l=0})
    \right),
  \label{eq:output_gate}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma$ is the sigmoid function.
This query-dependent gate (analogous to the G1 gate in AlphaFold~\cite{yin2025alphanet}) allows the model to selectively suppress or amplify each head's contribution.
When attention is active, degree-based normalization ($1/\sqrt{\deg}$) is replaced by the softmax normalization.

\subsubsection{Native ZBL zone bridging}
\label{sec:zbl_method}

\paragraph{ZBL pair energy.}
The Ziegler--Biersack--Littmark (ZBL) pair potential~\cite{ziegler1985} describes nuclear repulsion via a screened Coulomb form:
\begin{equation}
  E_{ij}^{\mathrm{ZBL}}(r_{ij}) =
    \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}
    \frac{Z_i Z_j e^2}{r_{ij}}\,
    \Phi\!\left(\frac{r_{ij}}{a_{\mathrm{ZBL}}}\right),
  \label{eq:zbl_pair}
\end{equation}
where $\Phi(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{4} c_k e^{-b_k x}$ is the universal screening function with fixed coefficients $(c_k, b_k)$ from Ref.~\cite{ziegler1985} (specifically $c = (0.18175, 0.50986, 0.28022, 0.02817)$ and $b = (3.1998, 0.94229, 0.4029, 0.20162)$), and
\begin{equation}
  a_{\mathrm{ZBL}} =
    \frac{0.88534\,a_0}{Z_i^{0.23} + Z_j^{0.23}}
\end{equation}
is the screening length ($a_0 = 0.529\,\text{\AA}$ is the Bohr radius).
Each pair $(i,j)$ contributes $E_{ij}^{\mathrm{ZBL}}/2$ to atom $i$ under a symmetric neighbour list.

\paragraph{The invariance requirement and its challenges.}
A naive implementation adds $E^{\mathrm{ZBL}}$ after the NN output; this is insufficient for conservative forces because the NN branch still ``feels'' the short-range pair geometry through two leakage channels: (1) the \emph{direction channel}---even if the scalar distance is frozen, the bond direction $\hat{r}_{ij}$ still rotates, and equivariant features at $l>0$ pick up angular dependence; (2) the \emph{multi-hop channel}---a third atom $\ell$ connected to both frozen atoms $j, k$ propagates their geometry back to $j$ after two message-passing steps.

\paragraph{InnerClamp: closing the scalar channel.}
\texttt{InnerClamp} maps the true distance to an effective distance $\tilde{r}_{ij}$ via a $C^3$-continuous septic Hermite interpolant:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{r}(r) =
  \begin{cases}
    r_{\mathrm{in}} & r \leq r_{\mathrm{in}}, \\
    r_{\mathrm{in}} + (r_{\mathrm{out}} - r_{\mathrm{in}})\,
      h_{\mathrm{clamp}}(t) & r_{\mathrm{in}} < r < r_{\mathrm{out}}, \\
    r & r \geq r_{\mathrm{out}},
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:innerclamp}
\end{equation}
where $t = (r - r_{\mathrm{in}})/(r_{\mathrm{out}} - r_{\mathrm{in}})$ and
\begin{equation}
  h_{\mathrm{clamp}}(t) = 20t^4 - 45t^5 + 36t^6 - 10t^7.
\end{equation}
The polynomial satisfies $h_{\mathrm{clamp}}(0)=0$, $h_{\mathrm{clamp}}(1)=1$, and zero first through third derivatives at both endpoints, giving $C^3$ joint continuity.
Both the scalar distance \emph{and} the displacement vector are clamped (the vector is rescaled to preserve direction while matching the clamped length), so all downstream quantities---radial basis, cutoff envelope, Wigner-D inputs---see constant geometry for any frozen-zone pair.

\paragraph{Source Freeze Propagation Gate (SFPG): closing direction and multi-hop channels.}
A per-edge switching amplitude is produced by a $C^3$ septic polynomial:
\begin{equation}
  w(r) =
  \begin{cases}
    0 & r \leq r_{\mathrm{in}}, \\
    h_{\mathrm{sw}}(t) & r_{\mathrm{in}} < r < r_{\mathrm{out}}, \\
    1 & r \geq r_{\mathrm{out}},
  \end{cases}
  \quad
  h_{\mathrm{sw}}(t) = 35t^4 - 84t^5 + 70t^6 - 20t^7.
  \label{eq:sfpg}
\end{equation}
The per-node gate is the product of switch values over all outgoing edges:
\begin{equation}
  \eta_j = \prod_{e \in \mathcal{E}(j)} w(r_e).
  \label{eq:sfpg_gate}
\end{equation}
Because every factor $w \in [0,1]$ and $w(r_e)=0$ the instant any neighbour of $j$ enters the frozen zone, $\eta_j = 0$ exactly.
The gate $\eta_j$ is broadcast to all outgoing edges as a multiplicative weight on aggregated messages, applied at every aggregation site: the Geometric Initial Embedding, the Environment Initial Embedding, and the SO(2) convolution (both plain and attention-weighted paths).
In the attention path, $\eta_j$ enters as a per-source weight in the softmax numerator and denominator, preventing frozen-zone sources from contributing to the normalization.
By induction on block index, if $\eta_j = 0$ for any atom $j$ that participates in a short-range pair, then no atom at any depth can receive information about the frozen-pair geometry, so $E^{\mathrm{NN}}$ is strictly constant under frozen-zone motions.
This propagation guarantee holds for both the plain and attention-weighted aggregation paths.

\paragraph{Configuration and default window.}
The bridging window is $[r_{\mathrm{in}}, r_{\mathrm{out}}] = [0.8, 1.2]$\,\AA{} by default.
The lower edge $0.8$\,\AA{} is chosen at the shortest distances present in the training corpus (OMat24, OMol, MPtraj-class datasets), ensuring the sub-0.8\,\AA{} region is handled entirely by the analytical ZBL formula without NN extrapolation.
Common short bonds (O--H at $0.96$\,\AA{}, N--H at $1.01$\,\AA{}, C--H at $1.09$\,\AA{}) fall in the transition zone where $\mathrm{d}\tilde{r}/\mathrm{d}r$ is close to unity, preserving gradient magnitude.
The 0.4\,\AA{} window width keeps the maximum second derivative of $\tilde{r}$ at $\approx 24$\,\AA$^{-2}$, maintaining numerical stability during force-loss training.

\paragraph{Contrast with wrapper-style ZBL.}
In wrapper-style implementations (e.g.\ DPA3 or MACE-MP-0), the ZBL energy is added to the NN prediction as a post-hoc correction.
This has two failure modes: (i) the NN branch has been trained without short-range data and may produce unphysical energies in the ZBL-dominant regime; and (ii) without the SFPG gate, the NN output still carries direction and multi-hop information from frozen pairs, so the combined potential is not conservative in the rigorous sense.
In DPA4, InnerClamp and SFPG together close all three leakage channels before differentiation.
Forces and virials obtained by \texttt{torch.autograd} are therefore the true negative gradient and stress of $E = E^{\mathrm{ZBL}} + E^{\mathrm{NN}}(\tilde{r})$ by construction.

\subsubsection{Symmetry guarantees}
\label{sec:symmetry}

\emph{Translational invariance} follows because all interatomic vectors $\mathbf{R}_j - \mathbf{R}_i$ are translation-invariant, and both $E^{\mathrm{NN}}$ and $E^{\mathrm{ZBL}}$ depend on positions only through such vectors.
\emph{Permutation equivariance} holds because $f_{\mathrm{msg}}$ and $f_{\mathrm{upd}}$ are identical for all pairs of the same species and the aggregation is a sum.
\emph{Rotational invariance} follows from two steps: (a) the Wigner-D rotation $D(\mathbf{q}_{ij})$ transforms messages equivariantly under SO(3), so the aggregated node feature transforms as an SO(3) tensor of the same $l$-type; and (b) the energy readout MLP acts only on the $l=0$ (invariant) subspace of the final node feature.
\emph{Conservativeness} holds for both branches: since $E^{\mathrm{NN}}$ and $E^{\mathrm{ZBL}}$ are summed as scalars before differentiation, forces $\mathbf{F}_i = -\partial E/\partial\mathbf{R}_i$ and the virial are the true negative gradient and stress of the same scalar energy function.
\emph{PES smoothness.}
The $C^3$ cutoff envelope ensures that all edge contributions, their forces, and their force derivatives vanish at $r_c$.
The InnerClamp interpolant is also $C^3$, so the combined PES is at least $C^3$ in atomic coordinates everywhere within the cutoff.

\subsection{Training}

DPA4 is trained end-to-end by minimising a weighted sum of energy MAE, force MAE, and virial MAE using a custom hybrid optimiser (\textbf{HybridMuon}).
Most weight matrices (2-D parameter tensors including \texttt{ChannelLinear}, \texttt{SO2Linear}, \texttt{FocusLinear}) are updated by the \textbf{Muon} optimiser~\cite{jordan2024muon} with \texttt{muon\_mode="slice"}: 3-D \texttt{SO3Linear} weights $(l_{\max}+1, C_{\mathrm{in}}, C_{\mathrm{out}})$ are sliced along the degree axis so each $l$-block receives an independent Newton--Schulz update with correct rectangular scaling.
Normalisation scales, layer scales, radial-basis frequencies, and type embeddings (prefixed \texttt{adam\_}) are routed to Adam without weight decay.
The Muon learning rate is controlled entirely by the global schedule; the \texttt{lr\_adjust} multiplier is set to~1 so no additional scaling is introduced.

Optionally, \textbf{Magma-lite damping} is applied to Muon-updated blocks: a per-block cosine-alignment score between the Muon momentum $m_t$ and the current gradient $g_t$ is EMA-smoothed ($\alpha_{\mathrm{EMA}} = 0.9$) and mapped to a scale in $[0.1, 1.0]$ via a temperature sigmoid ($\tau = 2.0$), reducing the update magnitude on blocks with noisy or misaligned gradients.
No stochastic block masking is used (unlike the original Magma paper), keeping damping dense and stable for force-field objectives.

\subsection{Inference and \texttt{torch.compile}}

DPA4 achieves an end-to-end \texttt{torch.compile} training path for the conservative-force objective, where the force loss requires second-order coordinate derivatives ($\partial^2 E/\partial\mathbf{R}\,\partial\theta$).
Standard \texttt{torch.compile} cannot handle \texttt{autograd.grad} with \texttt{create\_graph=True} because the second backward is opaque to Inductor.
DPA4 resolves this by first capturing the inner \texttt{autograd.grad} call as ordinary FX nodes with \texttt{make\_fx(tracing\_mode="symbolic")}, then handing the resulting flat graph to Inductor (\texttt{dynamic=True}).
The \texttt{make\_fx} pass also strips \emph{detach-chain} nodes that \texttt{make\_fx} inserts around saved activations under \texttt{create\_graph=True} and that would otherwise sever gradient flow, and rebuilds the FX graph from scratch to avoid stale C-level pointers left by \texttt{Graph.erase\_node}.

To maintain a stable compiled signature, the model uses a \emph{padded-neighbor representation} (a fixed-size dense neighbor tensor filled with dummy entries where the physical neighbor count falls short of the maximum) so that all tensor shapes are static at compile time.
The compiled callable is stored outside the \texttt{nn.Module} tree via \texttt{object.\_\_setattr\_\_} to prevent FSDP2/DDP from seeing duplicate parameter views.
A multi-slot compile cache keyed on \texttt{(training, do\_atomic\_virial, has\_coord\_corr)} allows seamless switching between training and validation without evicting the other slot's Inductor product.

Together these changes yield a \textbf{2--3$\times$} wall-clock speedup over eager training at the same model size, while preserving the full conservative-force guarantee.
At inference time, a separate Triton kernel suite (\texttt{DP\_TRITON=1}) fuses the Wigner-D rotation and radial-basis computation into a single pass, providing additional throughput improvement over the compiled eager path.
