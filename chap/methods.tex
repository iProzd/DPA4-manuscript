\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Datasets}

The inorganic-crystal benchmark trains DPA4 on MPtrj, the Materials Project
trajectory dataset introduced with CHGNet. MPtrj contains relaxation and static
calculations for inorganic crystals across 89 elements, with energies, forces,
stresses and magnetic moments computed at the GGA or GGA+$U$
level~\cite{deng2023chgnet,jain2013materials}. Generalisation to metastability
ranking is evaluated on the Matbench Discovery benchmark and its WBM test
set~\cite{riebesell2025matbench,WBM}. The architectural ablations use the same
WBM-subsampled protocol as the main inorganic-crystal experiments so that
changes in accuracy and throughput reflect the controlled model component.

The molecular benchmark uses SPICE-MACE-OFF, the organic-molecule data set used
for MACE-OFF. It combines SPICE, molecular-dynamics conformations,
QMugs-derived larger molecules and water clusters; reference energies and
forces are evaluated at the $\omega$B97M-D3(BJ)/def2-TZVPPD level with
PSI4~\cite{kovacs2023mace,
    eastman2023spice,eastman2024nutmeg,isert2022qmugs,najibi2018nonlocal,
    grimme2010consistent,grimme2011effect,weigend2005balanced,rappoport2010property,
    smith2020psi4}.

\subsection{DPA4 model architecture}

Consider a system of $N$ atoms with atomic numbers $\{Z_i\}$ and positions
$R=\{\mathbf{R}_i\}_{i=1}^{N}$. DPA4 represents the potential energy as the sum
of a learned equivariant message-passing branch and an analytical
Ziegler--Biersack--Littmark (ZBL) short-range branch:
\begin{equation}
    E(Z,R)=E_{\Theta}^{\mathrm{NN}}(Z,R) + E^{\mathrm{ZBL}}(Z,R),
    \label{eq:dpa4-total-energy}
\end{equation}
where $r_{ij}=|\mathbf{R}_j-\mathbf{R}_i|$ denotes the interatomic distance.
Forces and virials are obtained by differentiating the scalar energy in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:dpa4-total-energy} with respect to atomic positions and the
cell, so the neural and analytical branches define one conservative potential.

DPA4 carries node features in the real irreducible representation space
$V_{\leq L}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{C}$, where $V_{\leq L}=\bigoplus_{l=0}^{L}V_l$
and $V_l$ is the $(2l+1)$-dimensional irreducible representation of SO(3). The
$l=0$ subspace is invariant and is used for scalar readout; $l>0$ subspaces
transform equivariantly and carry angular information. Each interaction block
consists of a multi-focus SO(2) convolution in an edge-local gauge and an
equivariant feed-forward network. The architecture combines an Environment
Initial Embedding that injects a scalar local-environment prior at layer 0,
edge-conditioned radial degree coupling inside the local SO(2) frame,
envelope-gated attention for smooth aggregation, a Lebedev $S^2$
activation in the feed-forward branch and Native ZBL Zone Bridging for
short-range repulsion. Geometry-dependent quantities, including radial basis
functions, cutoff envelopes, local gauges and bridging gates, are constructed
once per neighbour graph and reused across blocks.

\subsubsection{Geometric inputs to the learned branch}
\label{sec:geom-inputs}

Throughout Section~\ref{sec:methods}, every geometric quantity entering the
learned branch $E_{\Theta}^{\mathrm{NN}}$ -- radial basis functions, cutoff
envelopes, real spherical harmonics $Y_l^m(\widehat{\mathbf{r}}_{ij})$, the
per-edge local-frame rotation $\mathbf{D}_{ij}$, and the local-environment
descriptor $\mathcal{D}_i$ -- is evaluated on the \emph{clamped distance}
$\widetilde{r}(r_{ij})$ in place of the raw $r_{ij}$. The unit direction
$\widehat{\mathbf{r}}_{ij}=\mathbf{r}_{ij}/r_{ij}$ is preserved exactly,
because the clamp acts purely on the radial magnitude. With this convention
the symbol $r_{ij}$ in every downstream equation should be read as
$\widetilde{r}(r_{ij})$ whenever it feeds the learned branch.

The map $\widetilde{r}:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$ is a $C^3$
piecewise function on a bridging window $[r_{\mathrm{in}},r_{\mathrm{out}}]$:
$\widetilde{r}(r)=r_{\mathrm{in}}$ for $r\leq r_{\mathrm{in}}$,
$\widetilde{r}(r)=r$ for $r\geq r_{\mathrm{out}}$, and a septic Hermite
interpolant connects the two on the window. The formal definition together
with the companion source-freeze gate is given in
Section~\ref{sec:zbl_method}.

Two consequences are used implicitly in the rest of Section~\ref{sec:methods}.
First, on the inner zone $r_{ij}\leq r_{\mathrm{in}}$ every NN-side geometric
quantity is constant in $r_{ij}$, so the learned branch contributes zero
gradient there and the short-range repulsion is handled exclusively by
$E^{\mathrm{ZBL}}$. Second, outside the bridging window
($r_{ij}\geq r_{\mathrm{out}}$) the clamp is the identity, so the learned
branch sees the true geometry.

\subsubsection{Geometric initial embedding (GIE)}
\label{sec:gie}

The initial feature $\mathbf{h}_i^{(0)}\in V_{\leq L}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{C}$
depends on both \emph{chemistry} (atomic species $Z_i$ and the species of
neighbours) and \emph{geometry} (the relative positions $\{\mathbf{r}_{ij}\}$
inside the cutoff). The two sources of information enter the $l=0$ and $l\geq 1$
slices in complementary ways.

\paragraph{$l=0$ slice.}
The chemistry-only baseline is a learnable element embedding
$\mathbf{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_t\times C}$, so that
$\mathbf{h}_{i,l=0,c}^{(0)}=\mathbf{T}_{Z_i,c}$ at first pass. To inject local
geometry into the scalar slice without breaking SO(3) invariance, DPA4 builds a
compact rotation-invariant local-environment descriptor $\mathcal{D}_i$ in the
spirit of the smooth-edition Deep Potential descriptor~\cite{zhang2018end} as
follows. Define the per-edge four-vector
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{u}_{ij,0} = \frac{s_5(r_{ij})}{r_{ij}},
    \qquad
    \mathbf{u}_{ij,k} = \mathbf{u}_{ij,0}\,\widehat{\mathbf{r}}_{ij,k},\quad k=1,2,3,
    \label{eq:dpa4-env-vector}
\end{equation}
whose first component is a smooth invariant and whose last three components
together transform as an SO(3) vector. Here $s_5$ is a particular instance of
the family of $C^3$ cutoff envelopes
\begin{equation}
    s_p(r) =
    \begin{cases}
        1+x^p\bigl(a_p+b_p x+c_p x^2+d_p x^3\bigr), & x=r/r_{\mathrm{c}}\in[0,1), \\
        0,                                          & x\ge 1,
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:dpa4-envelope}
\end{equation}
with coefficients $(a_p,b_p,c_p,d_p)$ uniquely fixed by
$s_p(r_{\mathrm{c}})=s_p'(r_{\mathrm{c}})=s_p''(r_{\mathrm{c}})=s_p'''(r_{\mathrm{c}})=0$
(closed forms given in Supplementary Section~\ref{sec:sm-radial}). DPA4 uses
$s_5$ for edge weighting and inside the smooth degree, and $s_7$ inside the
radial basis (Eq.~\eqref{eq:dpa4-radial-basis} below). With a separate
radial-species map
$\mathbf{g}:\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}\times\{1,\dots,n_t\}^2\to\mathbb{R}^{C_{\mathrm{env}}}$,
form the per-atom matrix
\begin{equation}
    A_i = n_i\sum_{j:r_{ij}<r_{\mathrm{c}}} \eta_j\,
    \mathbf{u}_{ij}\otimes \mathbf{g}(r_{ij};Z_i,Z_j)
    \in\mathbb{R}^{4\times C_{\mathrm{env}}},
    \label{eq:dpa4-env-matrix}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
    n_i = (d_i+\varepsilon)^{-1/2},
    \qquad
    d_i = \sum_{j:r_{ij}<r_{\mathrm{c}}} s_5(r_{ij})^2,
    \label{eq:dpa4-degree}
\end{equation}
is a smooth degree normalisation (squaring $s_5$ makes $d_i$ inherit $C^6$
regularity; the regulariser $\varepsilon>0$ prevents singularity for isolated
atoms), and $\eta_j$ is the source-freeze gate defined in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:dpa4-source-freeze}. Contracting the first axis of $A_i$ yields
a rotation-invariant Gram-style descriptor,
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{D}_i = A_i^{\top} A_i^{(:,1{:}K_{\mathrm{env}})}
    \in\mathbb{R}^{C_{\mathrm{env}}\times K_{\mathrm{env}}},
    \label{eq:dpa4-env-descriptor}
\end{equation}
where the truncation to $K_{\mathrm{env}}$ columns controls cost. Invariance of
$\mathcal{D}_i$ follows because the spatial part of $\mathbf{u}_{ij}$
transforms as an SO(3) vector while the scalar part is invariant, so $A_i^\top
A_i$ contracts the spatial index and the temporal component contributes a
scalar block. The descriptor then conditions the scalar features through a
Feature-wise Linear Modulation~\cite{perez2018film} (FiLM) step,
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{h}_{i,l=0}^{(0)}
    \leftarrow
    \boldsymbol{\gamma}_i \odot \mathbf{h}_{i,l=0}^{(0)} + \boldsymbol{\beta}_i,
    \label{eq:dpa4-film}
\end{equation}
with per-channel scale and shift
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\gamma}_i = \mathbf{1} + e^{\lambda_\alpha}\tanh\!\bigl(N_0(W_\alpha\,\mathrm{vec}\,\mathcal{D}_i)\bigr),
    \qquad
    \boldsymbol{\beta}_i = e^{\lambda_\beta}\tanh\!\bigl(N_0(W_\beta\,\mathrm{vec}\,\mathcal{D}_i)\bigr),
    \label{eq:dpa4-film-gb}
\end{equation}
where $W_\alpha,W_\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times C_{\mathrm{env}}K_{\mathrm{env}}}$
are learnable projections, $N_0$ is a scalar RMS normaliser, and the bounded
nonlinearities are gated by learnable log-strengths
$\lambda_\alpha,\lambda_\beta\in\mathbb{R}$ initialised at
$\lambda_\alpha=\lambda_\beta=\log(0.01)$, so the conditioning begins close to
the identity ($\boldsymbol{\gamma}_i\approx\mathbf{1}$,
$\boldsymbol{\beta}_i\approx\mathbf{0}$) and the species embedding dominates at
the start of training. Because $\mathcal{D}_i$ is SO(3)-invariant and FiLM acts
diagonally in the channel index, the $l=0$ slice remains a scalar.

\paragraph{$l\geq 1$ slices.}
For higher degrees no chemistry-only baseline exists: equivariant features must
carry directional information from the start. DPA4 obtains them by projecting
each neighbour direction onto real spherical harmonics and weighting the
projection by a radial-species profile,
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{h}_{i,\iota(l,m),c}^{(0)}
    \mathrel{+}=
    n_i\sum_{j:r_{ij}<r_{\mathrm{c}}}
    \eta_j\,Y_l^m(\widehat{\mathbf{r}}_{ij})\,
    \rho_{ij,l,c},\qquad l\geq 1.
    \label{eq:dpa4-gie}
\end{equation}
Here $\iota(l,m)$ indexes the coefficient of degree $l$ and order $m$, and
$n_i$ and $\eta_j$ are the same smooth degree normalisation and source-freeze
gate as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:dpa4-env-matrix} (Eqs.~\eqref{eq:dpa4-degree} and
\eqref{eq:dpa4-source-freeze}). The radial-species profile $\rho_{ij,l,c}$
mixes the per-pair chemistry into a smooth function of distance,
\begin{equation}
    \rho_{ij,l,c}
    =
    \bigl[\Pi^{\mathrm{rad}}\!\bigl(\boldsymbol{\phi}(r_{ij})\bigr)\bigr]_{l,c}
    + \bigl[\mathbf{T}_{\mathrm{edge}}(Z_i,Z_j)\bigr]_{c},
    \label{eq:dpa4-rho}
\end{equation}
where $\Pi^{\mathrm{rad}}:\mathbb{R}^{n_r}\to\mathbb{R}^{(L+1)\times C}$ is a
bias-free SiLU MLP, $\mathbf{T}_{\mathrm{edge}}(Z_i,Z_j)\in\mathbb{R}^{C}$ is a
per-pair species embedding broadcast across the $(L+1)$ degree slots, and
$\boldsymbol{\phi}(r_{ij})=(\phi_1(r_{ij}),\dots,\phi_{n_r}(r_{ij}))
\in\mathbb{R}^{n_r}$ is the sinusoidal radial basis
\begin{equation}
    \phi_n(r) = \frac{\sin(\omega_n r)}{r}\,s_7(r),\qquad n=1,\dots,n_r,
    \label{eq:dpa4-radial-basis}
\end{equation}
with learnable frequencies initialised at $\omega_n=n\pi/r_{\mathrm{c}}$ and
$s_7$ the cutoff envelope from Eq.~\eqref{eq:dpa4-envelope}.

Equation~\eqref{eq:dpa4-gie} is SO(3)-equivariant because every prefactor is
rotation-invariant and the entire angular content is carried by the degree-$l$
spherical harmonic $Y_l^m(\widehat{\mathbf{r}}_{ij})$, which transforms as
$D^l(R)$ under a rotation $R$.

\subsubsection{SO(2) multi-focus convolution}
\label{sec:so2}

Each interaction block applies an SO(3)-equivariant convolution
$\mathcal{C}_{\theta}$ that takes the per-atom node features
$\mathbf{h}_j\in V_{\le L}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{C}$, $j=1,\dots,N$, and returns
a per-atom update $(\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\mathbf{h})_i\in
V_{\le L}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{C}$ obtained by aggregating information from the
neighbours of atom $i$. The operator is built in six stages, developed below:
(i) transport each source node feature into a per-edge SO(2) gauge aligning
the bond direction with a fixed reference axis; (ii) construct an in-frame
edge feature through radial degree coupling, a multi-focus split, and a
multi-layer SO(2) stack; (iii) reweight the focus streams by a cross-focus
competition; (iv) lift the in-frame feature back to the global frame as the
per-edge equivariant message; (v) aggregate neighbour messages with
envelope-gated attention modulated by a destination-side output gate; and
(vi) project the result back to representation width through a channel
post-mixer. The closed form of $\mathcal{C}_{\theta}$ collecting all six
stages is given as Eq.~\eqref{eq:dpa4-conv} at the end of the subsection.

\paragraph{Edge-local frame.}
Full SO(3)-equivariant tensor products require Clebsch--Gordan expansions
whose cost grows steeply with angular
order~\cite{batatia2022mace,liao2023equiformerv2}. DPA4 instead reduces SO(3)
convolutions to SO(2) by rotating each directed edge $(i,j)$ into a gauge that
aligns its bond direction with the reference axis~\cite{passaro2023reducing},
\begin{equation}
    R_{ij}\widehat{\mathbf{r}}_{ij}=(0,0,1)^{\top}.
    \label{eq:dpa4-local-gauge}
\end{equation}
In this frame the residual symmetry is the abelian group SO(2), so angular
orders $m$ decouple into independent strata. DPA4 retains coefficients with
$|m|\leq M\leq L$ inside the convolution and applies a degree-dependent lift
factor $\Xi_M$ after rotating back to compensate the norm loss from truncation.

\paragraph{Per-edge equivariant message.}
For each directed edge $(i,j)$, the source node feature $\mathbf{h}_j$ is
transported into the edge-local frame and yields
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{x}_{ij}=P_M\,D(R_{ij})\,\mathbf{h}_j',
    \qquad
    \mathbf{h}_j'\;\coloneqq\;L^{\mathrm{pre}}_{\mathrm{deg}}\mathbf{h}_j
    \in V_{\le L}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{H},
    \label{eq:dpa4-local-projection}
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{x}_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}^{D_M\times H}$, where
$L^{\mathrm{pre}}_{\mathrm{deg}}$ is a degree-wise channel pre-mixer that
lifts the representation width from $C$ to a hidden width $H=F\,C_f$ (with
focus count $F$ and per-focus width $C_f$), $\mathbf{h}_j'$ is the pre-mixed
node feature at hidden width, $D(R_{ij})$ is block diagonal in $l$, and
$P_M$ selects the retained $m$-strata to dimension $D_M$. Let
$\widetilde{\rho}_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}^{(L+1)\times H}$ be the radial-species
feature $\rho_{ij}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:dpa4-rho} lifted from representation
width $C$ to hidden width $H$ by a degree-wise channel map
$L^{\mathrm{rad}}_{\mathrm{lift}}:\mathbb{R}^{C}\to\mathbb{R}^{H}$ applied
independently at each degree $l$. In the local frame the edge angular feature
$Y_l(\widehat{\mathbf{r}}_{ij})$ collapses to its $m=0$ component for every
degree $l$, so $\widetilde{\rho}_{ij}$ serves as the radial-modulated $m=0$
slice of the per-degree edge SO(2) irreps. A multiplication between these
edge-side and the node-side SO(2)-equivariant irreps $\mathbf{x}_{ij}$ then
yields, at each fixed $|m|$-stratum, a linear map that mixes the different
angular degrees $l$ without coupling different $|m|$,
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{x}_{ij,l,m,c}\;\leftarrow\;
    \sum_{l'\geq |m|}
    \mathcal{K}_{l,l',|m|,c}(\widetilde{\rho}_{ij})\,\mathbf{x}_{ij,l',m,c},
    \label{eq:dpa4-radial-coupling}
\end{equation}
where, for each $(l,l',|m|,c)$ with $c\in\{1,\dots,H\}$,
$\mathcal{K}_{l,l',|m|,c}(\widetilde{\rho}_{ij})\in\mathbb{R}$ is a scalar
coefficient and is a learnable linear function of $\widetilde{\rho}_{ij}$. To
keep the parameter count tractable when the hidden width $H$ is large,
$\mathcal{K}$ is parameterised by a low-rank factorisation across $c$,
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{K}_{l,l',|m|,c}(\widetilde{\rho}_{ij})
    =
    \sum_{r=1}^{R}
    K^{(r)}_{l,l',|m|}(\widetilde{\rho}_{ij})\,B_{r,c},
    \label{eq:dpa4-K-lowrank}
\end{equation}
with rank $R\leq H$, learnable scalar coefficients
$K^{(r)}_{l,l',|m|}(\widetilde{\rho}_{ij})\in\mathbb{R}$ (each a linear
functional of $\widetilde{\rho}_{ij}$), and a learnable channel basis
$B\in\mathbb{R}^{R\times H}$. The diagonal special case
$\mathcal{K}_{l,l',|m|,c}(\widetilde{\rho}_{ij})=\widetilde{\rho}_{ij,l,c}\,
\delta_{l,l'}$ reduces to per-degree scalar radial modulation. Because
$\mathcal{K}$ depends only on rotation-invariant radial-species information and
never mixes different $|m|$, the $(-m,+m)$ pair continues to transform as a
single two-dimensional real SO(2) representation.

The hidden width factorises as $\mathbb{R}^{H}=\mathbb{R}^{F}\otimes
\mathbb{R}^{C_f}$, so $\mathbf{x}_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}^{D_M\times F\times C_f}$
splits into $F$ \emph{focus streams}. A multi-layer SO(2) stack
$\mathcal{S}_{\Theta}$ acts in parallel on each focus,
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{x}_{ij}\;\leftarrow\;\mathcal{S}_{\Theta}(\mathbf{x}_{ij}).
    \label{eq:dpa4-so2-stack}
\end{equation}
The stack is a composition of $S$ residual layers, each of the form
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{x}_{ij}\;\leftarrow\;
    \mathbf{x}_{ij}+\Lambda_s\odot
    \Gamma_s\!\bigl(L^{\mathrm{SO2}}_s\,N_s(\mathbf{x}_{ij})\bigr),
    \qquad s=1,\dots,S,
    \label{eq:dpa4-so2-layer}
\end{equation}
where $N_s$ is an equivariant RMS norm, $L^{\mathrm{SO2}}_s$ is an
edge-independent SO(2)-equivariant linear map (unrestricted on $m=0$, the
real form of complex multiplication on each $|m|>0$ subspace) that supplies
cross-$l$ mixing at fixed $|m|$, $\Gamma_s$ is a gated activation acting on
the scalar slice, and $\Lambda_s\in\mathbb{R}^{F\times C_f}$ is a learnable
per-(focus,\,channel) residual scale initialised at $10^{-3}$. The parameter tuple
$\Theta=(L^{\mathrm{SO2}}_s,N_s,\Gamma_s,\Lambda_s)_{s=1}^{S}$ collects all
learnable weights of the stack.

\paragraph{Cross-focus competition.}
The focus streams compete through a softmax over the SO(2)-invariant $l=0$
slice. Let $\mathbf{x}^{(0)}_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}^{F\times C_f}$ be the
$(l,m)=(0,0)$ component of $\mathbf{x}_{ij}$ at the entry of the stack
\eqref{eq:dpa4-so2-stack}, and let
$N_0:\mathbb{R}^{C_f}\to\mathbb{R}^{C_f}$ be a focus-wise scalar RMS norm
applied independently to each of the $F$ stream rows. The per-focus
competition weight is
\begin{equation}
    \alpha_{ij,f}
    =
    (1-\epsilon)\,\frac{\exp\!\bigl(\tau^{-1}\sum_{c}W^{\mathrm{cf}}_{c,f}\,
        N_0(\mathbf{x}^{(0)}_{ij})_{f,c}\bigr)}
    {\sum_{f'}\exp\!\bigl(\tau^{-1}\sum_{c}W^{\mathrm{cf}}_{c,f'}\,
        N_0(\mathbf{x}^{(0)}_{ij})_{f',c}\bigr)}
    +\frac{\epsilon}{F},
    \label{eq:dpa4-focus-compete}
\end{equation}
where $W^{\mathrm{cf}}\in\mathbb{R}^{C_f\times F}$ is a learnable
channel-to-focus scoring matrix, $\tau>0$ is a softmax temperature that
sharpens ($\tau\to 0$) or flattens ($\tau\to\infty$) the competition between
streams, and $\epsilon\in[0,1)$ is a label-smoothing strength that mixes the
softmax with the uniform distribution $1/F$ over focuses to prevent any
single stream from being driven to zero focus weight. The weights then reweight
the focus streams,
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{x}_{ij}\;\leftarrow\;\alpha_{ij}\odot\mathbf{x}_{ij},
    \label{eq:dpa4-focus-reweight}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}^{F}$ broadcast across the $(l,m)$ and $c$
axes. Equivariance is preserved because $\alpha_{ij,f}$ is constructed from
an SO(2)-invariant $l=0$ slice.

\paragraph{Lift back to the global frame.}
The equivariant edge message in the global frame is recovered by inverting
the local gauge,
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{m}_{ij}
    =
    \Xi_M\,D(R_{ij})^{\top}P_M^{\top}\,\mathbf{x}_{ij}
    \in V_{\le L}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{H},
    \label{eq:dpa4-edge-message}
\end{equation}
with $D(R_{ij})^{\top}$ the inverse gauge rotation, $P_M^{\top}$ the
re-embedding of the truncated $m$-strata back into the full $(L+1)^2$ layout,
and $\Xi_M$ a degree-dependent rescale that compensates for the norm loss
from the $|m|\le M$ truncation. At this point $\mathbf{m}_{ij}$ still carries
the hidden width $H$; channel post-mixing back to representation width is
deferred to after neighbour aggregation.

\paragraph{Envelope-gated attention.}
\label{sec:attn}
The aggregation of $\mathbf{m}_{ij}$ over neighbours uses an envelope-gated
attention weight $w_{ij}^{(f,a)}$: a destination-wise normalised softmax over
neighbours whose logits are scalar functions of the invariant $l=0$
destination and source features plus a radial bias. Each focus $f\in\{1,\dots,F\}$
is split into $H_a$ heads of width $d_a=C_f/H_a$, indexed by
$a\in\{1,\dots,H_a\}$. Let $\mathbf{h}_n'$ be the pre-mixed hidden-width node
feature from Eq.~\eqref{eq:dpa4-local-projection}, whose $l=0$ slice
$\mathbf{h}_n'|_{l=0}\in\mathbb{R}^{H}$ can be reshaped along the
factorisation $H=F\cdot H_a\cdot d_a$ into
$\mathbb{R}^{F\times H_a\times d_a}$.
Applying a focus-wise scalar RMS norm $N_0$ in this reshaped layout gives
$N_0\bigl(\mathbf{h}_n'|_{l=0}\bigr)\in\mathbb{R}^{F\times H_a\times d_a}$,
from which we define per-edge per-(focus, head) queries and keys
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{q}_i^{(f,a)}
    = Q^{(f)}\,N_0\bigl(\mathbf{h}_i'|_{l=0}\bigr)_{f,a,:},\qquad
    \mathbf{k}_j^{(f,a)}
    = K^{(f)}\,N_0\bigl(\mathbf{h}_j'|_{l=0}\bigr)_{f,a,:}
    \in\mathbb{R}^{d_a},
    \label{eq:dpa4-attn-qk}
\end{equation}
with learnable per-focus query/key matrices $Q^{(f)},K^{(f)}\in
\mathbb{R}^{d_a\times d_a}$. The attention logit for edge $(i,j)$ at focus
$f$, head $a$ combines a scaled dot product with a radial bias linear in the
$l=0$ lifted radial-species feature $\widetilde{\rho}_{ij,0,c}$,
\begin{equation}
    \ell_{ij}^{(f,a)}
    =
    \frac{\langle\mathbf{q}_i^{(f,a)},\mathbf{k}_j^{(f,a)}\rangle}{\sqrt{d_a}}
    + \sum_{c=1}^{C_f}W^{\mathrm{rb}}_{c,f,a}\,\widetilde{\rho}_{ij,0,c},
    \label{eq:dpa4-attn-logit}
\end{equation}
where $W^{\mathrm{rb}}\in\mathbb{R}^{C_f\times F\times H_a}$ is a learnable
radial-bias tensor. The attention weight is
\begin{equation}
    w_{ij}^{(f,a)}
    =
    \frac{s_5(r_{ij})^2\,\eta_j\,\exp\!\bigl(\ell_{ij}^{(f,a)}\bigr)}
    {\operatorname{softplus}(\zeta_{f,a})
        +\sum_{k:r_{ik}<r_{\mathrm{c}}}
        s_5(r_{ik})^2\,\eta_k\,\exp\!\bigl(\ell_{ik}^{(f,a)}\bigr)},
    \label{eq:dpa4-attention}
\end{equation}
with the $C^3$ envelope $s_5$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:dpa4-envelope}, the
source-freeze gate $\eta_j$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:dpa4-source-freeze}, and a
learnable null-logit $\zeta_{f,a}\in\mathbb{R}$. (In the implementation the
standard max-subtraction stabilisation is applied to the exponents.) Two
mechanisms make $w_{ij}^{(f,a)}$ smooth at the cutoff: the numerator factor
$s_5(r_{ij})^2$ drives the weight $C^3$-smoothly to zero as
$r_{ij}\to r_{\mathrm{c}}$, and the
$\operatorname{softplus}(\zeta_{f,a})$ term in the denominator keeps the
denominator strictly positive even when every incident edge of atom $i$ is
silenced ($s_5\to 0$ or $\eta\to 0$), removing $0/0$ indeterminacies. The
weight $w_{ij}^{(f,a)}$ is SO(3)-invariant by construction, so its use as a
per-(focus, head) reweighting preserves equivariance.

Reshaping the channel axis of the edge message
$\mathbf m_{ij}\in V_{\le L}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{H}$ along the focus/head
factorisation $H=F\cdot H_a\cdot d_a$ yields per-(focus,\,head) slices
$\mathbf m_{ij}^{(f,a)}\in V_{\le L}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{d_a}$, and aggregation
under the attention weights gives
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf A_i^{(f,a)}
    =\sum_{j:r_{ij}<r_{\mathrm{c}}}w_{ij}^{(f,a)}\,\mathbf m_{ij}^{(f,a)}
    \in V_{\le L}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{d_a}.
    \label{eq:dpa4-attn-agg}
\end{equation}
A destination-side scalar output gate then modulates each $(f,a)$ slice
multiplicatively,
\begin{equation}
    \widetilde{\mathbf A}_i^{(f,a)}
    = G_i^{(f,a)}\,\mathbf A_i^{(f,a)},
    \qquad
    G_i^{(f,a)}
    = \sigma\!\Bigl(
        \textstyle\sum_{c=1}^{C_f} W^{\mathrm{og}}_{c,f,a}\,
        N_0\bigl(\mathbf h_i'|_{l=0}\bigr)_{f,c}
    \Bigr)\in(0,1),
    \label{eq:dpa4-attn-gate}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma(t)=(1+e^{-t})^{-1}$ is the logistic sigmoid and
$W^{\mathrm{og}}\in\mathbb{R}^{C_f\times F\times H_a}$ is a learnable output-gate
tensor. Concatenating the gated slices
$\widetilde{\mathbf A}_i^{(f,a)}$ back along the channel axis recovers a
single hidden-width tensor in $V_{\le L}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{H}$ (the inverse
of the focus/head split used above eq.~\eqref{eq:dpa4-attn-agg}), which is
then fed into a degree-wise channel post-mixer
$L^{\mathrm{post}}_{\mathrm{deg}}:V_{\le L}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{H}\to
V_{\le L}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{C}$ projecting from hidden width back to
representation width.

\paragraph{The convolution in closed form.}
Combining the per-edge message $\mathbf{m}_{ij}$ from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:dpa4-edge-message}, the envelope-gated attention weight
$w_{ij}^{(f,a)}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:dpa4-attention}, the destination-side
output gate $G_i^{(f,a)}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:dpa4-attn-gate}, and the channel
post-mixer $L^{\mathrm{post}}_{\mathrm{deg}}$, the SO(2) multi-focus
convolution at atom $i$ is
\begin{equation}
    \bigl(\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\mathbf{h}\bigr)_i
    =
    L^{\mathrm{post}}_{\mathrm{deg}}\!\biggl[
        \operatorname{concat}_{(f,a)}\!\Bigl(
            G_i^{(f,a)}\!\!\sum_{j:r_{ij}<r_{\mathrm{c}}}
            w_{ij}^{(f,a)}\,\mathbf{m}_{ij}^{(f,a)}
        \Bigr)
    \biggr]
    \in V_{\le L}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{C},
    \label{eq:dpa4-conv}
\end{equation}
where the concatenation $\operatorname{concat}_{(f,a)}$ stacks the gated
per-$(f,a)$ aggregations along the channel axis according to the inverse of
the $H=F\cdot H_a\cdot d_a$ focus/head split. Equivariance follows because
$w_{ij}^{(f,a)}$ and $G_i^{(f,a)}$ are SO(3)-invariant scalars and every
other operation is either degree-wise or acts inside an
SO(2)-equivariant local frame. The post-mixer is zero-initialised so
$\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\equiv 0$ at the start of training.

\subsubsection{Equivariant feed-forward network with Lebedev \texorpdfstring{$S^2$}{S2} grid activation}
\label{sec:s2-method}

After each SO(2) convolution, DPA4 applies an equivariant feed-forward network
(FFN) $\mathcal{F}_{\theta_{\mathrm{FFN}}}$ with residual connection. The FFN
acts independently on every atom and respects SO(3)-equivariance by
sandwiching a nonlinearity between two degree-wise SO(3)-linear maps. The
nonlinearity is realised as a \emph{spherical-grid SwiGLU}: the $l\geq 1$
slice is projected from spherical-harmonic coefficients to function values on
a discrete set of points on $S^2$, processed by a point-wise SwiGLU MLP on
those points, and projected back to spherical-harmonic coefficients; the
$l=0$ slice is processed in parallel by an ordinary scalar SwiGLU. The
spherical grid is chosen as a Lebedev quadrature rule.

\paragraph{Architecture.}
Let $L^{\mathrm{ch}}_{\mathrm{in}}:V_{\le L}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{C}\to V_{\le L}
\otimes\mathbb{R}^{H_{\mathrm{FFN}}}$ and $L^{\mathrm{ch}}_{\mathrm{out}}:
V_{\le L}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{H_{\mathrm{FFN}}}\to V_{\le L}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{C}$
be degree-wise channel-mixing maps at FFN hidden width $H_{\mathrm{FFN}}$.
Writing $\mathbf{u}\coloneqq L^{\mathrm{ch}}_{\mathrm{in}}\mathbf{h}_i\in
V_{\le L}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{H_{\mathrm{FFN}}}$ for the lifted node feature,
the FFN update is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{F}_{\theta_{\mathrm{FFN}}}(\mathbf{h}_i)
    =
    L^{\mathrm{ch}}_{\mathrm{out}}\!\bigl[\Phi^{\mathrm{grid}}(\mathbf{u})
    +\Psi^{\mathrm{scalar}}(\mathbf{h}_i|_{l=0})\bigr],
    \qquad
    \mathbf{h}_i \;\leftarrow\; \mathbf{h}_i + \mathcal{F}_{\theta_{\mathrm{FFN}}}(\mathbf{h}_i),
    \label{eq:dpa4-ffn}
\end{equation}
where $\Phi^{\mathrm{grid}}$ is the spherical-grid SwiGLU nonlinearity acting
on the full lifted feature $\mathbf{u}$ (all degrees $l=0,\dots,L$), and
$\Psi^{\mathrm{scalar}}$ is an auxiliary scalar SwiGLU that consumes the
original $l=0$ slice $\mathbf{h}_i|_{l=0}\in\mathbb{R}^{C}$ and contributes
only to the $l=0$ slot of the sum inside the brackets. The output linear
$L^{\mathrm{ch}}_{\mathrm{out}}$ is zero-initialised so the residual update
starts at the identity at training time zero.

\paragraph{Lebedev quadrature on \texorpdfstring{$S^2$}{S2}.}
A Lebedev rule of algebraic order of accuracy $p\geq 2L$ (also referred to
as algebraic precision) is a finite set of points
$\{\mathbf{q}_a\}_{a=1}^{A}\subset S^2$ together with positive weights
$\{w_a\}_{a=1}^{A}$, normalised so that $\sum_{a}w_a=1$. The defining
property is that, for every spherical-harmonic product of total degree at most
$p$, the discrete sum $\sum_{a}w_a\,f(\mathbf{q}_a)$ equals the exact
spherical average $(4\pi)^{-1}\int_{S^2}f$. In particular, with the
real-form spherical harmonics in the ``norm'' convention
$Y_l^m:S^2\to\mathbb{R}$, the discrete orthogonality
\begin{equation}
    \sum_{a=1}^{A}w_a\,Y_{l}^{m}(\mathbf{q}_a)\,Y_{l'}^{m'}(\mathbf{q}_a)
    =\frac{\delta_{ll'}\delta_{mm'}}{2l+1},\qquad 0\le l,l'\le L,
    \label{eq:dpa4-lebedev-orth}
\end{equation}
holds exactly whenever the precision satisfies $p\ge 2L$. Choosing $p=2L$
minimises $A$. Compared with the latitude--longitude product grids of
EquiformerV2--EquiformerV3, the Lebedev rule uses substantially fewer
sample points at the same algebraic order of accuracy and incurs much smaller
numerical equivariance error at the $L$ relevant for this work
(Supplementary Sec.~\ref{sec:sm-s2}).

\paragraph{Coefficient--grid projection.}
For an irreducible feature $\mathbf{u}\in V_{\le L}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{H_{\mathrm{FFN}}}$
with coefficients $\mathbf{u}_{(l,m),c}$, the forward and inverse projections
to grid values $\{\mathbf{U}_{a,c}\}_{a=1,\dots,A}$ are
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{U}_{a,c}
    =\sum_{l=0}^{L}\sum_{m=-l}^{l}Y_l^m(\mathbf{q}_a)\,\mathbf{u}_{(l,m),c},
    \qquad
    \mathbf{u}_{(l,m),c}
    =(2l+1)\sum_{a=1}^{A}w_a\,Y_l^m(\mathbf{q}_a)\,\mathbf{U}_{a,c},
    \label{eq:dpa4-s2-projection}
\end{equation}
which are mutually inverse on the band-limited space spanned by
$\{Y_l^m\}_{l\le L}$ by virtue of Eq.~\eqref{eq:dpa4-lebedev-orth}. Both
projections are linear in $\mathbf{u}$ (resp.\ $\mathbf{U}$); their
projection matrices depend only on the precomputed $(\mathbf{q}_a, w_a)$ and
are cached as buffers.

\paragraph{Point-wise grid SwiGLU $\Phi^{\mathrm{grid}}$.}
Define the point-wise SwiGLU nonlinearity
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{SwiGLU}(\mathbf{z})
    \coloneqq\sigma(\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{gate}})\odot
    \mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{gate}}\odot\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{val}},
    \quad
    \mathbf{z}=(\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{gate}},\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{val}})
    \in\mathbb{R}^{2H_{\mathrm{FFN}}},
    \label{eq:dpa4-swiglu}
\end{equation}
which splits its input internally into a gate half
$\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{gate}}\in\mathbb{R}^{H_{\mathrm{FFN}}}$ and a value half
$\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{val}}\in\mathbb{R}^{H_{\mathrm{FFN}}}$ along the channel
axis and returns a vector in $\mathbb{R}^{H_{\mathrm{FFN}}}$. The grid
nonlinearity $\Phi^{\mathrm{grid}}$ then acts on the full lifted feature
$\mathbf{u}\in V_{\le L}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{H_{\mathrm{FFN}}}$ in three steps.
First, the entire coefficient tensor (all degrees $l=0,\dots,L$) is projected
to grid values via Eq.~\eqref{eq:dpa4-s2-projection},
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{U}_{a,c}
    =\sum_{l=0}^{L}\sum_{m=-l}^{l} Y_l^m(\mathbf{q}_a)\,\mathbf{u}_{(l,m),c},
    \quad a=1,\dots,A.
    \label{eq:dpa4-s2-to-grid}
\end{equation}
Second, a two-layer point-wise MLP with a SwiGLU nonlinearity acts at each
grid point independently,
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{V}_{a,:}
    = W_2\,\mathrm{SwiGLU}\!\bigl(W_1\,\mathbf{U}_{a,:}\bigr),
    \label{eq:dpa4-s2-pointwise}
\end{equation}
where $W_1\in\mathbb{R}^{2H_{\mathrm{FFN}}\times H_{\mathrm{FFN}}}$
expands to $2H_{\mathrm{FFN}}$ channels (which SwiGLU consumes as the gate
and value halves) and $W_2\in\mathbb{R}^{H_{\mathrm{FFN}}\times
H_{\mathrm{FFN}}}$ is a second learnable linear map mixing the SwiGLU output
channels at the same width $H_{\mathrm{FFN}}$. Third, the
processed grid is mapped back to coefficients via the inverse projection of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:dpa4-s2-projection},
\begin{equation}
    \Phi^{\mathrm{grid}}(\mathbf{u})_{(l,m),c}
    =(2l+1)\sum_{a=1}^{A}w_a\,Y_l^m(\mathbf{q}_a)\,\mathbf{V}_{a,c},
    \quad 0\le l\le L,\ -l\le m\le l.
    \label{eq:dpa4-s2-from-grid}
\end{equation}

The auxiliary scalar branch $\Psi^{\mathrm{scalar}}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:dpa4-ffn}
takes the original $l=0$ slice of $\mathbf{h}_i$ at representation width $C$
and produces a width-$H_{\mathrm{FFN}}$ scalar output via
\begin{equation}
    \Psi^{\mathrm{scalar}}(\mathbf{h}_i|_{l=0})
    =
    \mathrm{SwiGLU}\!\bigl(W_3\,\mathbf{h}_i|_{l=0}\bigr)
    \in\mathbb{R}^{H_{\mathrm{FFN}}},
    \label{eq:dpa4-ffn-scalar}
\end{equation}
with $W_3\in\mathbb{R}^{2H_{\mathrm{FFN}}\times C}$; the scalar output
contributes only to the $l=0$ slot of the sum
$\Phi^{\mathrm{grid}}(\mathbf{u})+\Psi^{\mathrm{scalar}}(\mathbf{h}_i|_{l=0})$
inside the brackets of Eq.~\eqref{eq:dpa4-ffn}.

\paragraph{Equivariance.}
The projection \eqref{eq:dpa4-s2-to-grid} is the evaluation of a band-limited
function in $V_{\le L}$ at the points $\mathbf{q}_a$, so under
$\mathbf{q}\mapsto R\mathbf{q}$ the grid values transform as
$\mathbf{U}(\mathbf{q})\mapsto\mathbf{U}(R^{-1}\mathbf{q})$; a point-wise
nonlinearity commutes with this argument-substitution action. Provided
$p\ge 2L$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:dpa4-lebedev-orth} makes the inverse projection
\eqref{eq:dpa4-s2-from-grid} exact on $V_{\le L}$, so the round trip
``coefficients\,$\to$\,grid\,$\to$\,point-wise SwiGLU\,$\to$\,coefficients''
preserves SO(3)-equivariance to numerical precision. The auxiliary scalar
branch $\Psi^{\mathrm{scalar}}$ acts only on the trivial $l=0$
representation and is therefore SO(3)-invariant; adding its output to the
$l=0$ slot of $\Phi^{\mathrm{grid}}(\mathbf{u})$ does not affect the higher
degrees. Composition with the degree-wise channel-mixers
$L^{\mathrm{ch}}_{\mathrm{in}},L^{\mathrm{ch}}_{\mathrm{out}}$ preserves
equivariance of $\mathcal{F}_{\theta_{\mathrm{FFN}}}$.

\subsubsection{Native ZBL Zone Bridging}
\label{sec:zbl_method}

The analytical branch uses the ZBL screened Coulomb pair
potential~\cite{ziegler1985},
\begin{equation}
    E_{ij}^{\mathrm{ZBL}}(r)=
    \frac{k_{\mathrm{e}}Z_iZ_j}{r}\,
    \Phi\!\left(\frac{r}{a_{ij}}\right),
    \qquad
    a_{ij}=\frac{0.88534\,a_0}{Z_i^{0.23}+Z_j^{0.23}},
    \label{eq:dpa4-zbl}
\end{equation}
with the universal screening function $\Phi$ given in Supplementary
Section~\ref{sec:sm-zone-bridging}. DPA4 does not treat this term as a
post-hoc wrapper. Instead, the learned branch and analytical branch are coupled
through Native ZBL Zone Bridging, which removes the direct learned short-range
pair channel while assigning the corresponding inner-zone pair interaction to
the analytical branch.

First, a $C^3$ clamped distance map $\widetilde{r}(r)$ connects a constant
inner zone to the identity outside the bridging window:
\begin{equation}
    \widetilde{r}(r)=
    \begin{cases}
        r_{\mathrm{in}}, & r\leq r_{\mathrm{in}},              \\
        r_{\mathrm{in}}+(r_{\mathrm{out}}-r_{\mathrm{in}})h_{\mathrm{c}}(t),
                         & r_{\mathrm{in}}<r<r_{\mathrm{out}}, \\
        r,               & r\geq r_{\mathrm{out}},
    \end{cases}
    \qquad
    t=\frac{r-r_{\mathrm{in}}}{r_{\mathrm{out}}-r_{\mathrm{in}}}.
    \label{eq:dpa4-clamp}
\end{equation}
The associated clamped displacement is
$\widetilde{\mathbf{r}}_{ij}=\widetilde{r}(r_{ij})\widehat{\mathbf{r}}_{ij}$.
The polynomial $h_{\mathrm{c}}$ is chosen to match a constant function at
$r_{\mathrm{in}}$ and the identity map at $r_{\mathrm{out}}$, including first
through third derivatives required for $C^3$ gluing.

Second, a source-freeze gate
\begin{equation}
    \eta_j=\prod_{i:r_{ji}<r_{\mathrm{c}}} w(r_{ji})
    \label{eq:dpa4-source-freeze}
\end{equation}
uses a separate $C^3$ switching amplitude $w$ that is exactly zero in the inner
zone and exactly one outside the bridging window. If atom $j$ has any neighbour
inside the frozen zone, then $\eta_j=0$ and all information sourced from $j$ is
removed from the geometric initial embedding, Environment Initial Embedding,
plain aggregation and attention aggregation. The clamp closes the scalar
distance channel, while the gate closes the direction and multi-hop propagation
channels. Thus the direct learned contribution associated with an inner-zone
pair has zero derivative with respect to that pair channel, while learned
many-body contributions from non-frozen neighbours remain part of the neural
potential.

\subsubsection{Symmetry guarantees}
\label{sec:symmetry}

DPA4 is invariant to translations because all geometric inputs are built from
relative displacements. It is permutation-equivariant at the feature level and
permutation-invariant at the energy level because all edge and node maps are
shared across atoms of the same species and neighbour aggregation is by
summation or scalar softmax aggregation. Rotational invariance follows because
the local-frame convolution, equivariant feed-forward network and initial
embedding all intertwine the SO(3) action on $V_{\leq L}$; the readout acts
only on the final $l=0$ invariant slice. Smoothness follows from the $C^3$
cutoff envelope, the $C^3$ bridging maps and the positive denominator in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:dpa4-attention}. Conservativeness follows from differentiating
the single scalar energy in Eq.~\eqref{eq:dpa4-total-energy}.

\subsection{Training}

DPA4 is trained by minimising a weighted sum of mean absolute errors in energy
and virials, together with the mean Euclidean norm of the atomic force-vector
residual:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}
    =
    \lambda_E
    \left\langle
    \frac{|E-\widehat{E}|}{N}
    \right\rangle
    +\lambda_F
    \left\langle
    \left\|\mathbf{F}_i-\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_i\right\|_2
    \right\rangle_i
    +\lambda_\Pi
    \left\langle
    \frac{\|\Pi-\widehat{\Pi}\|_1}{9N}
    \right\rangle .
    \label{eq:dpa4-loss}
\end{equation}
The benchmark models use the same training protocol: mixed-precision training with
full-precision geometric reductions, tensor-float matrix products where
available, a warmup--stable--decay (WSD) learning-rate schedule and the
HybridMuon optimiser~\cite{wen2024wsd,jordan2024muon,liu2025muon}. HybridMuon
routes matrix-valued hidden transformations to Muon updates and scalar,
normalisation or auxiliary parameters to Adam-family updates. Degree-indexed
equivariant tensors are updated in slice mode so that distinct representation
blocks are not flattened into a single matrix, and the Muon path uses
match-RMS scaling and Magma-lite alignment damping to keep the update scale
compatible with the Adam-family path~\cite{joo2026magma}. Complete model- and
dataset-specific hyperparameters are reported in Supplementary
Tables~\ref{tab:sm_main_ablation_config}--\ref{tab:sm_matbench_config}.

\subsection{Compiled conservative-force training}

DPA4 supports compiled conservative-force training. In conventional MLIP
training, force matching differentiates through the conservative force
$\mathbf{F}=-\partial E_{\Theta}/\partial\mathbf{R}$ and therefore requires the
mixed second derivative
$\partial^2E_{\Theta}/\partial\mathbf{R}\,\partial\Theta$, because the training
gradient differentiates through the coordinate derivative. This nested-autograd
structure has prevented previous conservative MLIP training loops from being
compiled end to end. DPA4 resolves this by tracing the energy-to-force
derivative with \texttt{make\_fx} into an ordinary tensor graph and lowering
the resulting graph with PyTorch Inductor~\cite{ansel2024pytorch2}. The
compiled graph contains both the energy evaluation and its coordinate
derivative, while the outer backward pass supplies the parameter derivative
required by force-loss training.

The implementation keeps the neighbour representation shape-stable and
preserves inactive neighbours as exactly silent contributions, so compiled
training retains the same conservative energy-to-force relationship as the
uncompiled model. In ablations, the compiled mixed-precision training path
gives the observed 2--3$\times$ wall-clock speedup without a systematic
accuracy penalty (Table~\ref{tab:dpa4_compile_ablation}).
