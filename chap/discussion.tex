\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

DPA4 shows that the deployability gap of equivariant MLIPs can be narrowed when
representation, aggregation, training and short-range physics are designed as
one energy-conservative system. The local-frame SO(2) construction retains
directional information in the message-passing pathway without the full cost of
SO(3) Clebsch--Gordan tensor products. The low-rank edge--node product,
multi-focus competition and envelope-gated attention then use this
representation efficiently, while the geometry-informed embedding, Lebedev-grid
nonlinearity and compiled conservative-force path reduce the optimisation and
systems overheads that often limit expressive equivariant models. This
co-design explains why DPA4 moves both the accuracy--parameter frontier across
inorganic crystals and organic molecules and the accuracy--cost frontier on
Matbench Discovery.

The practical implication is that high-accuracy MLIP development no longer has
to be a large-scale training exercise. DPA4-Pro reaches the leading compliant
Matbench Discovery CPS without DeNS or direct-force pretraining and at lower
A100-equivalent training cost than the DeNS-assisted EquiformerV3 baseline. At
the compact end, DPA4-Air reaches higher CPS than the much larger eSEN baseline
with 2.76M parameters and 42.9-fold less training compute. DPA4-Plus further
gives the lowest aggregate SPICE-MACE-OFF molecular energy and force errors
among the compared models, indicating that the architectural gains are not
limited to inorganic-crystal relaxation. Together with the approximately
3$\times$ acceleration from the compiled conservative-force pathway, these
results make compact, target-specific and energy-conservative potentials more
practical to train, ablate and redeploy within ordinary simulation workflows.

Native ZBL Zone Bridging addresses a complementary failure mode. It is not the
main source of the Matbench Discovery or SPICE-MACE-OFF gains; rather, it
targets close-contact regimes that are weakly represented in standard
near-equilibrium benchmarks. In the C--Si dimer scan, the tested external
DP-ZBL-style correction appears smooth in energy but produces a force-level
switching artifact near the splice region. By placing the analytical repulsion
inside the same scalar energy and suppressing the learned short-range pair
channel, DPA4 removes this artifact in the local test while retaining
energy-derived forces and a smooth transition to the learned regime. Long-time
molecular-dynamics tests are still needed to determine how this local
improvement translates to energy drift, collision stability and damage
evolution.

Several limitations remain. DPA4 is evaluated here as a single-task,
per-dataset MLIP architecture, not as a pretrained large atomistic model. Its
behaviour under large-scale multitask training, heterogeneous DFT datasets and
LAM-style pretraining remains to be established. The short-range study is also
intentionally local, and the present benchmarks do not yet test autonomous
active-learning convergence or closed-loop experimental deployment.

The natural next step is therefore to use DPA4 as a backbone for LAM
pretraining and downstream adaptation, while preserving conservative training
efficiency and native short-range coupling. In that setting, the relevant
question is not only whether a single pretrained model reaches a higher
benchmark score, but whether accurate target-domain potentials can be
generated, tested and refined repeatedly at low cost. More reliable and
accessible potential generation may in turn support more automated, agentic
workflows for building materials potentials, and eventually help connect
simulation-driven model updates with dry--wet closed-loop materials discovery.
