\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

DPA4 shows that the deployability gap of equivariant MLIPs narrows when representation, aggregation, training and short-range physics are co-designed as one energy-conservative system rather than assembled as independent components.
Carrying directional information through a local-frame SO(2) pathway avoids the full cost of SO(3) Clebsch--Gordan tensor products, while a compiler-friendly conservative-force path removes much of the optimisation and systems overhead that usually limits expressive equivariant models.
It is this co-design, rather than any single component, that moves DPA4 onto a better accuracy--parameter frontier across both inorganic crystals and organic molecules and a better accuracy--cost frontier on Matbench Discovery.

The practical implication is that competitive equivariant potentials no longer require a large-scale training budget.
Across both the materials and molecular benchmarks, DPA4 matches or surpasses substantially larger baselines and baselines trained with auxiliary DeNS or direct-force objectives, while being optimised only through the conservative energy path and at lower parameter count and training cost.
Together with the compiler-driven training acceleration, this makes compact, target-specific and energy-conservative potentials practical to train, ablate and redeploy within ordinary simulation workflows.

Native ZBL Zone Bridging targets a complementary failure mode rather than the benchmark accuracy itself.
By placing the analytical short-range repulsion inside the same scalar energy, it removes the force-level switching artifact that an external energy-level correction can introduce near the splice region, while keeping the forces conservative.
This was demonstrated only in a local dimer test, and long-time molecular-dynamics validation---energy drift, collision stability and damage evolution---remains future work.
More broadly, we establish DPA4 here as a generalist backbone architecture in the single-task, per-dataset setting; its realisation as a fully pretrained large atomistic model under large-scale multitask training and heterogeneous DFT datasets, together with autonomous active-learning convergence and closed-loop deployment, remains to be established.

The natural next step is therefore to use DPA4 as a backbone for LAM pretraining and downstream adaptation while preserving its conservative-training efficiency and native short-range coupling.
The relevant question is then not only whether a single pretrained model reaches a higher benchmark score, but whether accurate target-domain potentials can be generated, validated and refined repeatedly at low cost---an ingredient for more automated, agentic potential development and, ultimately, for closing the loop between computation and experiment, so that simulation-driven model updates and laboratory feedback iteratively refine one another.
