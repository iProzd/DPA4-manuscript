\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

DPA4 shows that equivariant accuracy and practical efficiency can be improved together when representation, aggregation and training are designed as one system.
The central architectural idea is the SO(2) local-frame construction, which keeps directional features in the message-passing pathway without relying on full SO(3) Clebsch--Gordan tensor products.
The surrounding design choices improve geometric initialization, message aggregation, conservative-force training and short-range coupling within the same energy-based framework.
This combination explains why the same MLIP architecture improves the accuracy--parameter frontier across both inorganic crystals and organic molecules.

The results also show that architecture design for MLIPs must account for engineering constraints and practical training efficiency, not only formal expressiveness.
DPA4-Air reaches the compliant Matbench Discovery frontier with single-digit A100-equivalent GPU-days, and the compiled conservative-force pathway further reduces the wall-clock cost of training.
This shifts specialised MLIP development towards the setting implied by on-demand interatomic potentials: a high-accuracy model for a target dataset can be trained in days on accessible single-GPU hardware rather than requiring a large dedicated training campaign.
The practical consequence is that accurate interatomic potentials can become routine tools for specific scientific problems, rather than infrastructure-scale modelling projects.

Native ZBL Zone Bridging is a robustness component rather than the main source of benchmark accuracy.
The C--Si dimer scan shows that coupling the analytical repulsion inside the scalar energy avoids the force-level switching artifact observed for an external pair correction.

Several limitations remain.
DPA4 is evaluated here as a single-task, per-dataset MLIP architecture, not as a pretrained large atomistic model.
Its behaviour under large-scale multitask training, heterogeneous DFT datasets and LAM-style pretraining remains to be established.
The short-range study is also intentionally local, and full extreme-condition molecular-dynamics tests remain future work.

The natural next step is to use DPA4 as a backbone for LAM pretraining and downstream adaptation.
Future work should optimise the core LAM workflow across multitask pretraining, downstream fine-tuning and efficient model distillation.
In the emerging era of agentic science, these directions point beyond a single model towards autonomous workflows that coordinate architecture exploration, interatomic potential generation, active data sampling and closed-loop simulations.
