\section{Introduction}

Atomistic simulation is shifting from a case-by-case analysis tool to a predictive engine for materials discovery, molecular design and non-equilibrium process modelling.
This shift changes what is expected from an interatomic potential: it must retain density-functional-theory (DFT) accuracy, remain stable in long molecular-dynamics trajectories, transfer across chemical environments and be inexpensive enough for high-throughput use.
Machine-learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs) have made this goal plausible by replacing repeated electronic-structure calculations with near-linear-cost surrogate potential-energy surfaces~\cite{behler2007generalized,bartok2010gaussian,zhang2018deep,batzner2022nequip,batatia2022mace}.
Early and widely used MLIP families achieve this by encoding each atomic neighbourhood through invariant descriptors or scalar message-passing features, including atom-centred neural-network potentials~\cite{behler2007generalized}, Gaussian approximation potentials~\cite{bartok2010gaussian}, SchNet~\cite{schutt2017schnet}, PhysNet~\cite{unke2019physnet}, Deep Potential~\cite{zhang2018deep,wang2018deepmd} and the DPA series~\cite{zhang2022dpa,zhang2024dpa2,zhang2025dpa3}.
The recent emergence of pretrained large atomistic models (LAMs), such as M3GNet~\cite{chen2022universal}, CHGNet~\cite{deng2023chgnet}, MatterSim~\cite{yang2024mattersim}, Orb~\cite{neumann2024orb,rhodes2025orbv3}, UMA~\cite{wood2025umafamilyuniversalmodels} and DPA3~\cite{zhang2025dpa3}, shows that large DFT corpora can support transferable potentials across broad regions of chemistry and materials space.

The next challenge is no longer accuracy alone, but the joint optimisation of accuracy, efficiency and physical robustness.
Equivariant architectures address the accuracy side of this problem by carrying directional information as first-class features instead of compressing it immediately into invariant scalars.
NequIP~\cite{batzner2022nequip}, Allegro~\cite{musaelian2022learning}, MACE~\cite{batatia2022mace}, Equiformer-family models~\cite{liao2023equiformerv2,liao2026equiformerv3} and eSEN~\cite{fu2025esen} have demonstrated that equivariance can substantially improve data efficiency and benchmark accuracy.
However, the most expressive SO(3)-equivariant models rely on Clebsch--Gordan tensor products or dense spherical-grid operations whose cost grows rapidly with angular order, model width and neighbour count.
This cost becomes a bottleneck when the target is not a single specialised potential, but an MLIP architecture that can scale to broad training sets and serve as the backbone of future LAMs.

Two additional constraints are becoming equally important.
First, modern conservative-force training is an implementation problem as much as a modelling problem: differentiating force losses through $E(R)$ requires mixed coordinate--parameter second derivatives, and dynamic neighbour graphs, in-place graph updates and Python-level control flow often prevent efficient compiler lowering.
Second, the short-range region of the potential-energy surface remains difficult for data-driven models.
Close atomic contacts in cascades, impacts, compression, melting or reactive transition paths are sparse in ordinary DFT training data; external Ziegler--Biersack--Littmark (ZBL) corrections can repair the asymptotic repulsion, but post-hoc wrappers may compromise the smoothness and conservativeness of the learned potential~\cite{ziegler1985}.
These limitations motivate an architecture that treats directional expressiveness, training efficiency and short-range physics as coupled design requirements rather than separate add-ons.

Here we present DPA4, an equivariant interatomic-potential architecture designed around these three requirements.
DPA4 combines local-frame equivariant message passing, compiler-friendly conservative-force training and native analytical short-range coupling in one model family.
Its main contributions are as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{SO(2)-equivariant local-frame architecture with attention.}
          For each directed edge $(i,j)$, DPA4 chooses an edge-local gauge whose $z$-axis aligns with the bond direction $\hat{r}_{ij}$.
          The residual rotation symmetry in this gauge is the abelian group SO(2), so equivariant operations become diagonal in the angular-order basis $\{e^{im\theta}\}$ and avoid full Clebsch--Gordan tensor products.
          The resulting local-frame update, inspired by the eSCN framework~\cite{passaro2023reducing}, keeps directional equivariant features while reducing the per-edge angular-message cost.
          DPA4 further couples this update to multi-head attention over SO(2)-invariant ($m=0$) channels, allowing each message-passing step to adapt its neighbourhood aggregation to many-body context.
    \item \textbf{End-to-end \texttt{torch.compile} training.}
          DPA4 combines the lower algebraic cost of SO(2) updates with a shape-stable neighbour representation and compiler-friendly tensor program.
          The conservative-force path requires differentiating forces through $E(R)$ and therefore includes second-order coordinate derivatives; DPA4 makes this path compilable by tracing the inner \texttt{autograd.grad} operation into ordinary FX nodes before lowering with PyTorch Inductor.
          In the experiments below, this compiled conservative-force path gives a \textbf{2--3$\times$} wall-clock training speedup over eager execution at matched accuracy and runs without model changes on commodity GPUs as well as data-centre accelerators.
    \item \textbf{Native ZBL Zone Bridging.}
          DPA4 incorporates the analytical ZBL branch inside the scalar energy rather than applying it as an external correction.
          A two-component \emph{Zone Bridging} framework removes the direct learned short-range pair channel while preserving learned many-body interactions from non-frozen neighbours: \textbf{InnerClamp} is a $C^3$ septic Hermite distance saturation that closes the scalar-distance leakage channel, and \textbf{SFPG} (Source Freeze Propagation Gate) is a smooth per-node multiplicative gate that closes the direction and multi-hop propagation channels when a neighbour enters the inner frozen zone.
          The resulting potential remains $C^3$ in atomic coordinates away from coincident atoms and assigns the singular inner-zone pair interaction to the physically motivated analytical branch.
\end{enumerate}
DPA4 variants form a new accuracy--efficiency frontier on Matbench Discovery~\cite{riebesell2025matbench}, as summarized by the CPS-versus-training-cost comparison in Fig.~\ref{fig:cps}, and improve the molecular benchmark errors on SPICE-MACE-OFF~\cite{kovacs2023mace}.
The largest DPA4 variant reaches state-of-the-art Matbench Discovery performance, while compact variants approach the accuracy of much larger baselines with substantially fewer parameters and lower training cost.
We present DPA4 here as a single-task, per-dataset MLIP architecture; multi-task dataset encoding and LAM pre-training are natural extensions rather than assumptions of the present study.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:results} first gives an overview of the DPA4 architecture, then reports its accuracy on Matbench Discovery and SPICE-MACE-OFF, its training and inference efficiency, its native ZBL coupling behaviour, and controlled ablations of the main architectural and systems components.
