\section{Introduction}

Atomistic simulation is increasingly being asked to operate as an engine for materials discovery, molecular design and non-equilibrium process modelling, not only as a post-processing tool for individual density functional theory (DFT) calculations.
This shift changes the requirement on an interatomic potential: it must retain quantum-mechanical fidelity, scale to long trajectories and large systems, transfer across chemical environments, and remain stable when the simulation visits configurations far from equilibrium.
Machine learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs) have made this program plausible by replacing repeated electronic-structure calculations with near-linear-cost surrogate potential-energy surfaces~\cite{behler2007generalized,bartok2010gaussian,zhang2018deep,batzner2022nequip,batatia2022mace}.
The emergence of large atomistic models trained on broad DFT corpora further suggests that transferable potentials can be learned at scale across molecules and materials~\cite{batatia2023foundation,zhang2024dpa2,zhang2025dpa3}.

Yet a general-purpose potential is limited not by accuracy alone, but by the joint constraints of expressiveness, trainability and physical robustness.
First, high-accuracy equivariant architectures such as NequIP~\cite{batzner2022nequip}, MACE~\cite{batatia2022mace} and EquiformerV2~\cite{liao2023equiformerv2} represent angular information explicitly, but full SO(3) tensor products carry a steep algebraic cost.
The Clebsch--Gordan contractions used in these models grow rapidly with the maximum spherical-harmonic order, making the most expressive versions difficult to scale to large models, broad training sets and long molecular-dynamics trajectories.
Second, modern conservative-force training is an implementation problem as much as a modelling problem: force losses require coordinate--parameter second derivatives, while variable neighbour graphs, in-place graph updates and Python-level control flow often prevent efficient lowering by modern compiler stacks.
Third, the short-range part of the potential-energy surface remains a weak point for data-driven models.
Close atomic contacts in cascades, impacts, compression, melting or reactive transition paths probe a regime that is sparsely represented in ordinary DFT training data; external Ziegler--Biersack--Littmark (ZBL) corrections can repair the asymptotic repulsion, but post-hoc wrappers may break the smoothness and conservativeness of the learned potential.

Here we present DPA4, a conservative equivariant interatomic-potential architecture designed around these three requirements.
DPA4 treats directional expressiveness, compiled conservative-force training and analytical short-range physics as one architectural problem rather than as independent add-ons.
Its main contributions are as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{SO(2)-equivariant local-frame architecture with attention.}
          For each directed edge $(i,j)$, DPA4 chooses an edge-local gauge whose $z$-axis aligns with the bond direction $\hat{r}_{ij}$.
          The residual rotation symmetry in this gauge is the abelian group SO(2), so equivariant operations become diagonal in the angular-order basis $\{e^{im\theta}\}$ and avoid full Clebsch--Gordan tensor products.
          The resulting local-frame update, inspired by the eSCN framework~\cite{passaro2023reducing}, keeps directional equivariant features while reducing the per-edge angular-message cost.
          DPA4 further couples this update to multi-head attention over SO(2)-invariant ($m=0$) channels, allowing each message-passing step to adapt its neighbourhood aggregation to many-body context.
    \item \textbf{End-to-end \texttt{torch.compile} training.}
          DPA4 combines the lower algebraic cost of SO(2) updates with a shape-stable neighbour representation and compiler-friendly tensor program.
          The conservative-force path requires differentiating forces through $E(R)$ and therefore includes second-order coordinate derivatives; DPA4 makes this path compilable by tracing the inner \texttt{autograd.grad} operation into ordinary FX nodes before lowering with PyTorch Inductor.
          In the experiments below, this compiled conservative-force path gives a \textbf{2--3$\times$} wall-clock training speedup over eager execution at matched accuracy and runs without model changes on commodity GPUs as well as data-centre accelerators.
    \item \textbf{Native ZBL Zone Bridging.}
          DPA4 incorporates the analytical ZBL branch inside the scalar energy rather than applying it as an external correction.
          A two-component \emph{Zone Bridging} framework removes the direct learned short-range pair channel while preserving learned many-body interactions from non-frozen neighbours: \textbf{InnerClamp} is a $C^3$ septic Hermite distance saturation that closes the scalar-distance leakage channel, and \textbf{SFPG} (Source Freeze Propagation Gate) is a smooth per-node multiplicative gate that closes the direction and multi-hop propagation channels when a neighbour enters the inner frozen zone.
          The resulting potential remains $C^3$ in atomic coordinates away from coincident atoms and assigns the singular inner-zone pair interaction to the physically motivated analytical branch.
\end{enumerate}
DPA4 variants form a competitive accuracy--efficiency frontier on Matbench Discovery~\cite{riebesell2025matbench} and improve the molecular benchmark errors on SPICE-MACE-OFF in the experiments reported here.
Training-efficiency ablations further show that the compiled conservative-force path is responsible for the 2--3$\times$ wall-clock speedup.
We present DPA4 here as a single-task, per-dataset architecture; multi-task dataset encoding and foundation-model pre-training are natural extensions rather than assumptions of the present study.
