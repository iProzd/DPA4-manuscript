\subsection{Mechanism ablations}
\label{sec:ablation}

To establish that the accuracy--efficiency gains originate from the proposed
mechanisms rather than from confounding hyperparameter choices, we vary one
component at a time under a matched training protocol and evaluate each variant
on a subsample of the WBM test set~\cite{WBM}. We summarise the principal
comparison for each mechanism below and report the complete sweeps, protocols
and configurations in Supplementary Section~\ref{sec:sm-ablation}.

Envelope-gated softmax attention improves neighbourhood aggregation without
altering the equivariant feature space. In the deployed two-focus configuration,
attention-weighted aggregation lowers the energy and force MAEs by 9.2\% and
6.8\%, respectively, relative to scatter-sum aggregation, for a 6\% increase in
training time (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:dpa4_attention_ablation}). Because
the weights are computed from rotationally invariant scalar channels, this gain
reflects adaptive neighbour weighting rather than any relaxation of SO(3)
equivariance.

Multi-focus SO(2) convolution separates expressivity from raw channel width. At
a fixed SO(2) dimension of 192, the 96-channel two-focus model matches the
accuracy of the 192-channel single-focus model (energy and force MAEs of
26.994~meV/atom and 36.408~meV/\AA{} versus 27.286~meV/atom and 36.477~meV/\AA{})
while using 56\% fewer parameters, 23\% less training time and 34\% less
inference time. Widening the single stream beyond this point yields no further
benefit: the 256-channel single-focus model carries about four times as many
parameters yet attains higher errors (28.409~meV/atom and 36.670~meV/\AA{}),
indicating that a single wide equivariant stream is harder to optimise to
comparable quality under a shared training recipe
(Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:dpa4_focus_ablation}). Allocating the same angular
budget to focus channels that compete over distinct edge-local motifs therefore
provides a more parameter-efficient and more readily optimised route for scaling
the SO(2) representation than widening a single stream.

Radial degree coupling adds complementary expressivity inside the local SO(2)
frame. A compact rank-1 per-channel degree-mixing kernel lowers the energy and
force MAEs by 3.1\% and 6.9\%, respectively, relative to scalar radial scaling,
at only 1.12$\times$ the training time
(Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:dpa4_radial_degree_ablation}). This low-rank kernel
provides the most favourable accuracy--throughput trade-off and serves as the
default radial coupling.

The remaining studies in Supplementary Section~\ref{sec:sm-ablation}---spanning
compiled mixed-precision training, interaction depth, attention design variants,
normalisation placement, the learning-rate schedule and the $S^2$ quadrature
rule---corroborate this conclusion: the reported improvements arise from the
targeted architectural mechanisms, while the other sweeps establish stable
operating points for the released DPA4 variants.
