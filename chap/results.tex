\subsection{The DPA4 architecture}
\label{sec:arch_overview}

DPA4 is a conservative SO(3)-equivariant message-passing graph neural network for interatomic potentials, built around an SO(2) local-frame transformation and attention-based neighbour aggregation; see Section~\ref{sec:methods} for the full formulation.

Figure~\ref{fig:arch} summarizes the model at three levels.
Panel (a) shows the overall energy model, in which Geometry-Aware Input Embedding (GIE), stacked equivariant interaction blocks and an atomic energy head form a single differentiable energy path.
Panel (b) focuses on GIE, which injects local geometric information into the initial representation using cached neighbour geometry and radial features.
Panel (c) shows the SO(2) multi-focus convolution, where local-frame equivariant messages are constructed and aggregated by Smooth Gated Attention during message passing.

The key architectural choice is to perform equivariant message passing in an edge-local SO(2) representation rather than through full SO(3) Clebsch--Gordan tensor products; see Section~\ref{sec:so2} and Supplementary Section~\ref{sec:sm-cg-trivialisation} for details.
GIE is applied once at the input stage to initialise scalar and higher-degree node features from local geometry, as described in Section~\ref{sec:initial-representation}.
Each subsequent interaction block rotates source features into the edge-local SO(2) frame, applies multi-focus SO(2) convolution, lifts the resulting edge messages back to the global frame, and aggregates them with Smooth Gated Attention, implemented as the envelope-gated softmax attention in Section~\ref{sec:attn}.
After the interaction blocks, the invariant $l=0$ features are passed to the atomic energy head, while forces and virials are obtained by differentiating the total energy.

Beyond the message-passing architecture, two design choices improve practical training efficiency and short-range robustness.
First, the conservative force path is implemented in a compiler-friendly form compatible with \texttt{torch.compile}, as detailed in Section~\ref{sec:methods}.
Second, DPA4 includes a native ZBL branch through a smooth Zone Bridging mechanism for close contacts; see Section~\ref{sec:zbl_method}.
The optimisation procedure uses a WSD learning-rate schedule~\cite{wen2024wsd} and the HybridMuon optimiser: Muon updates are applied to matrix-valued hidden transformations~\cite{jordan2024muon,liu2025muon}, while scalar, normalisation and auxiliary parameters use Adam-family updates~\cite{kingma2014adam}.
Further optimisation details are given in Supplementary Sections~\ref{sec:sm-wsd-schedule}--\ref{sec:sm-hybrid-muon}.

Matbench Discovery is a widely used benchmark for evaluating machine-learning models in high-throughput inorganic-crystal discovery~\cite{riebesell2025matbench}.
In the compliant setting, models are trained on MPtrj~\cite{deng2023chgnet}, used to relax WBM candidate structures~\cite{WBM}, and then evaluated by their formation-energy predictions and derived convex-hull distances.
The resulting scores combine classification, regression and structural-relaxation metrics, making the benchmark a stringent test of both energy accuracy and relaxation quality.
Here we focus on the compliant leaderboard to keep the training data fixed across models and more directly compare architectural differences.
Non-compliant leaderboard entries may additionally involve different training datasets or fine-tuning strategies and are left for future comparison.

Table~\ref{tab:matbench} reports the Matbench Discovery leaderboard metrics, while Fig.~\ref{fig:cps} shows the corresponding accuracy--efficiency frontier in terms of CPS versus training cost.
DPA4-Pro establishes the best CPS in Table~\ref{tab:matbench}, reaching 0.833 with an F1 score of 0.859, a $\kappa$SRME of 0.255 and 20.91M parameters.
Its CPS is slightly higher than EquiformerV3+DeNS-MP (0.833 versus 0.830) while using 31\% fewer parameters (20.91M versus 30.3M)~\cite{liao2026equiformerv3,liao2024dens}.
As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:cps}, DPA4-Pro also reaches this accuracy with a lower A100-equivalent training cost than EquiformerV3+DeNS-MP.

This comparison is notable because several high-ranking baselines use additional accuracy-enhancing training stages, marked by dashed marker outlines in Fig.~\ref{fig:cps}.
DeNS has been shown to improve equivariant force fields by denoising non-equilibrium structures~\cite{liao2024dens}, and EquiformerV3+DeNS-MP further combines this strategy with direct-force pretraining~\cite{liao2026equiformerv3}.
DPA4-Pro uses neither DeNS nor direct-force training; it is trained through the conservative energy path and still surpasses the DeNS-assisted EquiformerV3 baseline in CPS.
This result suggests that the DPA4 architecture itself contributes to the improved accuracy rather than relying on an auxiliary denoising or direct-force objective.

The smaller DPA4 variants show that the same architecture remains effective in a lower-cost regime.
DPA4-Plus reaches a CPS of 0.822 with only 5.40M parameters, reducing the gap to EquiformerV3+DeNS-MP to 0.008 CPS while using 82\% fewer parameters.
DPA4-Air reaches a CPS of 0.804 with 2.76M parameters, exceeding eSEN-30M-MP (0.797 CPS, 30.1M parameters) with a 10.9$\times$ smaller model~\cite{fu2025esen}.
DPA4-Neo contains only 1.60M parameters but still reaches a CPS of 0.781, comparable to MatRIS-10M-MP (0.778 CPS, 10.4M parameters) with a 6.5$\times$ smaller model~\cite{zhou2026matris}.
Beyond parameter count, Fig.~\ref{fig:cps} compares the A100-equivalent training cost needed to reach each point on the leaderboard.
On this scale, DPA4-Air requires only single-digit A100 GPU-days, 42.9$\times$ less training compute than eSEN-30M-MP while achieving a higher CPS.
For perspective, the A100 has a peak FP32 throughput of 19.5~TFLOPS, whereas recent single-card workstation GPUs can exceed 100~TFLOPS peak FP32 throughput.
Under an idealized peak-FLOP rescaling, the DPA4-Air training budget is therefore on the order of one day on such hardware.
These compact variants are important for high-throughput workflows because they offer strong accuracy with lower training cost and faster deployment.

As the leading compliant models are now separated by relatively small CPS differences, further optimisation of the fixed-MPtrj Matbench Discovery leaderboard may have limited practical value once models reach the present accuracy--efficiency frontier.
This does not diminish the usefulness of the benchmark; rather, it suggests that future comparisons may be more informative when they also consider non-compliant settings with broader DFT training datasets, additional fine-tuning data or different deployment objectives.

\subsection{Small-molecule benchmarks}
\label{sec:small_mol}

DPA4 was next evaluated on SPICE-MACE-OFF, the organic-molecule benchmark used
by MACE-OFF~\cite{kovacs2023mace}. The benchmark spans PubChem molecules,
DES370K~\cite{donchev2021des370k} monomers and dimers, dipeptides, solvated
amino acids, water clusters and larger QMugs-derived
molecules~\cite{eastman2023spice,isert2022qmugs}. Reference energies and forces
were computed at the $\omega$B97M-D3(BJ)/def2-TZVPPD
level~\cite{eastman2023spice,kovacs2023mace}. We use the same
train/validation/test split as the MACE-OFF and DPA3
comparisons~\cite{kovacs2023mace,zhang2025dpa3} and report per-subset energy
and force MAEs. We also report a logarithmic weighted average MAE (LWAMAE),
which summarizes performance across chemically heterogeneous subsets without
allowing a single high-error subset to dominate the aggregate score.

Table~\ref{table:spice} shows that DPA4 improves molecular accuracy across all
subsets. DPA4-Plus gives the lowest energy and force MAEs in every subset and
reduces the aggregate LWAMAE to 0.09~meV/atom for energies and 1.77~meV/\AA{}
for forces. Relative to the 6.5M-parameter eSEN baseline~\cite{fu2025esen},
this corresponds to approximately 36\% lower energy LWAMAE and 31\% lower force
LWAMAE with a smaller 5.3M-parameter model. Relative to
DPA3-L24~\cite{zhang2025dpa3}, the reductions are approximately 59\% and 69\%,
respectively.

The smaller DPA4-Air model also performs competitively. With 2.7M parameters,
it already improves on the 6.5M-parameter eSEN model in aggregate energy and
force LWAMAEs and is second-best in most subset-level comparisons. The
improvement is especially pronounced for water, solvated amino acids and QMugs,
where DPA4-Air substantially lowers the energy error relative to DPA3-L24 while
remaining close to the larger DPA4-Plus model. Together, the two DPA4 sizes
show that DPA4 remains highly parameter- and data-efficient across both
inorganic crystals and chemically diverse molecular data.

\subsection{Training and inference efficiency}
\label{sec:efficiency}

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig/lmp-inorganic_500.png}
    \caption{
        LAMMPS inference throughput on the LAMBench \texttt{inorganic\_500}
        test~\cite{peng2025lambench}.
        Each point reports molecular-dynamics throughput for periodic
        repetitions of the same inorganic seed structure, evaluated with the
        LAMMPS--DeePMD-kit interface~\cite{thompson2022lammps,zeng2025deepmdv3}
        after warm-up on an NVIDIA H20 GPU.
        The four panels show complementary speed and cost measures; higher
        rates and lower time-per-ns or time-per-step values indicate faster
        simulation.
    }
    \label{fig:lmp_inorganic_infer}
\end{figure}

The training efficiency of DPA4 is quantified by the CPS--training-cost
comparison in Fig.~\ref{fig:cps}, using the Matbench Discovery CPS
metric~\cite{riebesell2025matbench}. DPA4-Air reaches a CPS of 0.804 using 7.8
A100 GPU-days, compared with 0.797 using 335 A100 GPU-days for
eSEN-30M-MP~\cite{fu2025esen}; this corresponds to a 43$\times$ lower training
cost with a slightly higher score. DPA4-Plus raises the CPS to 0.822 at 41 A100
GPU-days, an 8.2$\times$ lower cost than eSEN-30M-MP, and DPA4-Pro reaches a
CPS of 0.833 at 106.6 A100 GPU-days, slightly above EquiformerV3+DeNS-MP
(0.830)~\cite{liao2026equiformerv3} at a 1.5$\times$ lower cost. These
comparisons are conservative with respect to the training protocol: the
high-CPS dashed-outline baselines in Fig.~\ref{fig:cps} use additional training
strategies such as DeNS~\cite{liao2024dens} or direct-force
pretraining~\cite{fu2025esen,liao2026equiformerv3}, whereas all DPA4 variants
are trained throughout with conservative forces obtained as gradients of a
scalar energy. Incorporating analogous DeNS-style pretraining may further
reduce the DPA4 training cost or increase the CPS, but is not used in the
results reported here. The conservative training path is made practical by the
compiled implementation: in controlled ablations,
\texttt{torch.compile}~\cite{ansel2024pytorch2} together with bf16 automatic
mixed precision provides a 3.1$\times$ wall-clock training speedup and reduces
peak training memory to about 40\% of the FP32 baseline
(Table~\ref{tab:dpa4_compile_ablation}).

Deployment-oriented inference was evaluated through the LAMMPS--DeePMD-kit
interface, which is the relevant path for production molecular dynamics. The
primary comparison uses the LAMBench \texttt{inorganic\_500} system-size sweep
and therefore includes neighbour-list construction, force evaluation and
simulation driver overhead, rather than timing isolated neural-network kernels.
The LAMMPS benchmarks were run with Kokkos acceleration~\cite{trott2022kokkos}
on a single NVIDIA H20 GPU; full software details and additional ASE/LAMMPS
measurements are given in Supplementary
Figs.~\ref{fig:sm_lmp_catalysts}--\ref{fig:sm_ase_catalysts}.

Figure~\ref{fig:lmp_inorganic_infer} shows that compact DPA4 models preserve
high molecular-dynamics throughput while using the same equivariant
architecture that gives the Matbench Discovery gains above. As expected, the
scalar DPA1-L0 baseline~\cite{zhang2018deep} remains the fastest model, but it
represents a substantially less expressive accuracy regime. Among the more
accurate neural potentials, DPA4-Neo and DPA4-Air maintain favourable atom-step
throughput up to $10^4$ atoms. At the largest systems, DPA4-Neo delivers
roughly twice the atom-step throughput of MACE-S and several times that of
MACE-M~\cite{batatia2022mace,barroso2024open}, while DPA4-Air retains a smaller
but consistent advantage over the MACE baselines. Thus the compact DPA4
variants are not only cheaper to train; they also provide a practical inference
regime for large inorganic simulations.

\subsection{Native ZBL coupling and extreme conditions}
\label{sec:zbl}

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{fig/dimer_c_si.pdf}
    \caption{
        Short-range C--Si dimer response for models trained on an
        ABACUS-computed 3C-SiC data set~\cite{li2016abacus}.
        The DPA3 baseline is a five-layer DPA3 model trained with the
        DeePMD DP-ZBL pairwise correction~\cite{wang2019dpzbl}, whereas
        DPA4 uses the Native ZBL Zone Bridging branch described in
        Section~\ref{sec:zbl_method}. The analytical curve is the
        ZBL screened Coulomb potential~\cite{ziegler1985}.
    }
    \label{fig:zbl}
\end{figure}

We next test the short-range branch on a C--Si dimer scan derived from a 3C-SiC
data set computed with ABACUS. This one-dimensional probe is deliberately more
severe than ordinary validation structures: it forces the model into the
sub-\AA{} regime in which first-principles training labels are sparse and the
repulsive screened Coulomb interaction should dominate. The comparison includes
a five-layer DPA3 model trained with the DeePMD pair-ZBL strategy, which
smoothly interpolates a Deep Potential model with an empirical pair potential
for close contacts~\cite{wang2019dpzbl}, and the DPA4 model with the native ZBL
branch enabled.

Figure~\ref{fig:zbl} shows that both learned models give a smooth attractive
dimer energy over most of the scanned range, but the force curve exposes the
difference between external pair coupling and native scalar-energy coupling.
The DPA3 Pair ZBL baseline develops a sharp force excursion near the switching
region, producing a local attractive impulse even though the analytical ZBL
force is strongly repulsive at these separations. In contrast, DPA4 follows the
analytical ZBL force in the inner region and joins smoothly to the learned
force as the C--Si distance increases. This behaviour is the empirical
counterpart of the construction in Section~\ref{sec:zbl_method}: the analytical
ZBL branch reads the true distance, the learned branch reads a $C^3$ clamped
displacement, and the source-freeze gate removes the direct learned short-range
pair channel. As a result, short-range repulsion is supplied by the analytical
branch before the total scalar energy is differentiated, avoiding the
force-level switching artifact observed for the pair-ZBL baseline.
