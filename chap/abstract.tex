\begin{abstract}
    Machine-learning interatomic potentials now approach quantum-mechanical accuracy in many benchmarks, but routine model generation remains limited by the cost of expressive equivariant architectures, conservative-force training and short-range physical extrapolation.
    Here we introduce DPA4, the Smooth equivariant Zone-bridging Model (SeZM), an SO(3)-equivariant energy model built around local-frame SO(2) message passing, geometry-informed embeddings and native coupling to an analytical Ziegler--Biersack--Littmark repulsion.
    The architecture is compact and compiler friendly: all variants are trained through energy-derived forces, and \texttt{torch.compile} provides an approximately 3$\times$ wall-clock speedup without replacing conservative training by a direct-force surrogate.
    On the compliant Matbench Discovery benchmark, DPA4-Pro attains the best Combined Performance Score (CPS) among the compared models, whereas the 2.76M-parameter DPA4-Air matches or exceeds the accuracy of much larger eSEN baselines with 10.9-fold fewer parameters and 42.9-fold less training compute, while retaining practical ASE inference throughput.
    On SPICE-MACE-OFF, DPA4-Plus gives the lowest aggregate molecular energy and force errors among the compared models.
    DPA4 also incorporates ZBL close-contact repulsion within the same scalar energy, removing the force-switching artifact observed for an external short-range correction.
    Together, these results support DPA4 as a scalable backbone for accurate, conservative and deployable interatomic potentials.
\end{abstract}
