\begin{abstract}
    Machine-learning interatomic potentials are moving from specialised
    surrogates towards on-demand models that must be accurate across chemistry,
    stable under close atomic contacts and efficient enough to train at scale.
    Existing architectures usually improve one of these axes at the expense of
    another: highly equivariant models are costly, invariant models miss
    directional information, and post-hoc short-range corrections can introduce
    non-conservative forces. Here we report DPA4, also referred to as SeZM for
    Smooth equivariant Zone-bridging Model, a conservative atomistic model that
    integrates architectural, physical and training-system advances in a single
    scalar potential. The neural branch augments local-frame equivariant message
    passing with envelope-gated softmax attention, multi-focus SO(2)
    convolution, radial edge coupling and Lebedev-quadrature $S^2$ activation,
    improving angular and environmental expressivity at controlled computational
    cost. An optional short-range branch couples the learned potential to an
    analytical Ziegler--Biersack--Littmark repulsion through Zone Bridging, where
    a clamped learned geometry and a source-freeze gate remove the direct learned
    close-contact pair channel without an external wrapper. DPA4 also makes
    conservative force matching compatible with \texttt{torch.compile}, yielding
    2--3$\times$ wall-clock training acceleration in controlled ablations. Across
    inorganic-crystal and organic-molecule benchmarks, DPA4 sets new
    state-of-the-art results on Matbench Discovery and SPICE-MACE-OFF,
    establishing a practical foundation for accurate, efficient and physically
    robust on-demand interatomic potentials.
\end{abstract}
