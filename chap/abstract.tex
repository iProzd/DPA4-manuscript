\begin{abstract}
    Machine-learning interatomic potentials are becoming a practical route to on-demand atomistic simulation, but broad deployment requires models that are simultaneously accurate, efficient and stable beyond equilibrium.
    Existing architectures often trade one requirement for another: highly equivariant models are costly to train and evaluate, whereas faster models can lose directional fidelity and usually treat short-range physics as an external correction.
    Here we report DPA4, also referred to as SeZM for Smooth equivariant Zone-bridging Model, an equivariant interatomic potential based on an SO(2) convolution and attention framework.
    The model preserves directional information at moderate computational cost, incorporates a native short-range treatment, and supports \texttt{torch.compile}-compatible conservative-force training.
    Across inorganic-material and organic-molecule benchmarks, DPA4 achieves the best reported accuracy on both Matbench Discovery and SPICE-MACE-OFF among the compared models.
    A compact DPA4 variant reaches eSEN-level Matbench Discovery accuracy with roughly one tenth of the parameters and can be trained within a few GPU-days on a single GPU.
    These results establish DPA4 as a practical foundation for accurate, efficient and robust on-demand interatomic potentials.
\end{abstract}
