Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, ``Pushing the accuracy--cost frontier of machine-learning interatomic potentials,'' as a Research Article in \textit{Science Advances}.

Accurate atomistic models must express directional interactions while remaining affordable to train and evaluate repeatedly.
DPA4 addresses these requirements through a conservative equivariant architecture that combines coupling between edge and node angular degrees with a Wigner bilinear nonlinearity on the rotation group.
The full-frame Wigner bilinear construction has a universal approximation guarantee; the deployed models use an economical frame truncation, with quadrature chosen to preserve the nonlinearity's equivariance to floating-point precision.
A complementary compiled implementation makes training through energy-derived forces practical without auxiliary denoising or direct-force pretraining.

The manuscript connects these methodological choices to controlled ablations and evaluations across inorganic crystals and organic molecules.
On Matbench Discovery, all five evaluated DPA4 variants lie on the measured accuracy--throughput Pareto frontier, with one matching the strongest tested baseline's composite score at 11.9-fold higher saturated throughput.
The wider significance is a demonstrated reduction in the cost of learning and evaluating conservative potential-energy surfaces across distinct chemical domains.
We believe this combination of equivariant model design, physical consistency and computational efficiency will interest readers in materials science, computational chemistry and scientific machine learning.

This work has not been published in a peer-reviewed journal and is not under consideration elsewhere.
